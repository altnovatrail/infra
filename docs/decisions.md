# infra — decision log

> Why infra is built the way it is. Each entry: what was decided, why, what was rejected, and in its tail "— *Consequence:*" the files, by their paths, that a change of it edits.
>
> **Rule:** do not propose again what was rejected without new facts. If a decision has to change, ask the author and rewrite its entry in its place, and every entry the change touches: an entry is its text in force — what was decided, why, what was rejected and what a change of it edits —, git keeps the old text, and the commit says why. An entry's number stands in its heading alone: an entry cites no other entry and no section, says in words what it rests on, and carries no date of who decided it and no amendment; a fact that no run of ours checks cites its one record of [sources.md](sources.md) or [measurements.md](measurements.md).
>
> The repository was set up 2026-10-19 from the template of `spec` v0.1.0. How it comes to differ from the template — tools in the devcontainer, rules in RUN.md — is recorded here as a decision: the devcontainer has three tools more, OpenTofu, ShellCheck and actionlint. Next number — R38.
>
> Open questions that concern several repositories — О-, П-, Т- — are in the registers of `spec`, in `spec/docs/product.md`, `spec/docs/privacy.md` and `spec/docs/repositories.md`. What is still to decide in the component itself is in [architecture.md](architecture.md).

## The `spec` tag

**R01. `infra`'s documents are checked by `just -f spec/justfile docs-check-repo` of the `spec` its submodule pins, and a move to a tag of `spec` takes what came from the template as the tag has it — the devcontainer among it — whole, with only the differences this log records.**
- The submodule `spec/` pins the tag, and CLAUDE.md and RUN.md name it; no other document names the tag.
- The shared check "Documents / docs-check-repo" of `.github` builds the stage `toolchain` from the caller's `.devcontainer/Dockerfile` and runs this recipe in it, by the script of the caller's submodule: it checks the links, the log, the tables of the plan and the citations against `spec` at the submodule's tag.
- An example of a citation is written in inline code, so that the check does not take it for one.

— *Why:* the check is required for the merge, and a tag without its recipe would hold every merge; a link to a section that is not at the tag, or a gap in the log, would pass unnoticed. The container of `infra` is the container of `spec`, so a difference is a decision of its own, and nothing asks for one beyond the tools of this repository's code.

— *Rejected:*
- staying on a tag older than the check: the required check would be red and hold any merge;
- moving without the devcontainer, or carrying over its changes selectively: a difference would have to be recorded as a decision, and there is no reason for one;
- examples of citations in guillemets, «…»: the check matches a number in guillemets against the log.

— *Consequence:* `spec/`; `.devcontainer/`; CLAUDE.md; RUN.md; `docs-budgets.json`.

**R02. A move to a tag of `spec` goes to its latest release, immutable and accepted by `gh release verify`, at once and not through the tags between; the tasks of the plan that the tag's decisions answer are rewritten by them in the same move.**
- The move reads what the tags between bring this repository — the log of `spec` between the tags, the rows of `spec`'s table of debt that name `infra`, the component's card and RULES.md — and its commit says what was taken.
- A task that would prepare an answer to a question `spec` has decided by then decides only what `spec` left to `infra`. The statuses of the tasks are the author's, and a move does not change them.

— *Why:* an intermediate tag would take two merges of the template and two moves, with nothing to do on it; and a task that asks what `spec` has decided could propose what `spec` rejected.

— *Rejected:*
- a tag between the current one and the latest release: two merges of the template, and nothing to do on the intermediate one;
- leaving a task as it was, to prepare an answer to a question `spec` has since decided: it would ask what is decided;
- removing or putting off tasks of the plan because `spec`'s plan puts off this repository's work: the statuses of the tasks here are the author's;
- carrying over the changes of the devcontainer selectively: the container of `infra` is the container of `spec`.

— *Consequence:* `spec/`; CLAUDE.md; RUN.md; `run/`.

**R03. A decision that closes a question of `spec`'s registers is recorded in `spec`; `infra` records what follows from it for its own resources once its submodule stands on a tag that holds the decision, not before.**
- What follows here from a decision of `spec` — the place of each resource, the jobs and the facts of `api` at the deployment — is an entry of this log and the text of [architecture.md](architecture.md), on the tag that holds the decision.

— *Why:* a decision that closes a question of the register belongs to `spec`, and the documents here would otherwise rest on what the submodule's `spec` does not yet hold.

— *Rejected:*
- recording what follows from a decision before the tag, by a reference to the author's word and the row of table 3 that asked it: the decision is `spec`'s, and the submodule would not hold it;
- a tag that does not yet hold the decision.

— *Consequence:* `docs/decisions.md`; `docs/architecture.md`; `run/QUESTIONS.md`.

## Region

**R04. Everything `infra` keeps in a region is in `us-east4`: the Cloud Run services and jobs, the Artifact Registry repository, the secrets with user-managed replication, the log bucket with the sink `_Default`, and the trace bucket `_Trace`, whose place the project's default setting for Observability buckets fixes before the first trace. `_Required` stays in `global` while there is no Google Cloud organization.** The platform's region is the US, one place for all learners, as `spec` decided it; here is what it means for each resource of `infra`.
- **The region of each resource:**
  - the Cloud Run services and jobs — `us-east4`, beside Neon `aws-us-east-1` of the database of `api`: both are in Northern Virginia (sources 1.4, 1.6);
  - the Artifact Registry repository — regional, `us-east4`: the place of a repository does not change after it is created, and a multi-region is slower (sources 1.5);
  - the Secret Manager secrets — with user-managed replication, at the place `us-east4`. Automatic replication replicates data without restrictions, and a regional secret is accessible only from its own region, while CI on GitHub reads the keys for eval (sources 1.5);
  - the log bucket — its own, in `us-east4`, and the sink `_Default` is directed into it: Cloud Logging creates the bucket `_Default` in `global`, and its place does not change (sources 1.1);
  - the trace bucket `_Trace` — the module sets the project's default place for Observability buckets, `us-east4`, before the first trace, and the system creates the bucket with the first trace, at this place: otherwise the default settings or the system itself would choose it, and only a reprovision, which erases the traces, can change it later (sources 1.2). The module does not create the bucket itself;
  - `_Required` — in `global`: only a setting of the organization or of a folder made before the project gives it a region, and an organization is tied to a domain, and there is none until О-16 (sources 1.1, 1.3). The debt is Д3 in [run/DEBT.md](../run/DEBT.md).
- **Not here:** the region of the database of `api`, Neon `aws-us-east-1`, is kept by `api`; Firebase Auth processes data only in the US in any region, and Firebase Hosting is a global service (sources 1.7); the platform's region does not decide the region of the AI calls (sources 1.8).

— *Why:* the EU gives the first slice nothing — sign-in goes through the US, and there are no learners from the EU and the United Kingdom before a DPIA —, one place gives the admission function no new input, and `us-east4` has the Tier 1 prices of Cloud Run (sources 1.4). The setting rather than the bucket: the provider creates `google_observability_bucket` by a POST, which refuses if `_Trace` already exists — after a `destroy`, in a self-hosting project where traces already were, or if the first trace got ahead of `apply` —, and it cannot delete the bucket; the setting it sets by a PATCH, which a second `apply` repeats without harm (sources 2.2; [`resource_observability_bucket.go`](https://github.com/hashicorp/terraform-provider-google/blob/v8.10.0/google/services/observability/resource_observability_bucket.go) and [`resource_observability_project_settings.go`](https://github.com/hashicorp/terraform-provider-google/blob/v8.10.0/google/services/observability/resource_observability_project_settings.go) of the provider at v8.10.0).

— *Rejected:*
- the EU, and a region by learner: the platform has one region;
- the multi-region `us` for Artifact Registry and the buckets: there is one region anyway, and a multi-region repository is slower (sources 1.5);
- regional secrets: CI on GitHub, outside `us-east4`, would not read the key;
- leaving `_Trace` to the system's choice until the first trace: the system would choose the place, and changing it later loses the traces;
- an explicit `_Trace` bucket in the module: each of the cases above would stop `apply` until a manual `tofu import`; and both the bucket and the setting — the refusal of the explicit bucket would remain;
- setting up a Google Cloud organization before the first deployment, so that `_Required` stands in the region: it needs a domain, and the domain waits for О-16.

— *Cost:* the EU later means a new Google Cloud project, a new Neon project and new buckets, with the data moved: the place of none of them changes.

— *Consequence:* `tofu/logging.tf`; `tofu/registry.tf`; `tofu/secrets.tf`; `tofu/production.auto.tfvars`; `docs/architecture.md`; `run/DEBT.md`.

## Rollout

**R05. The rollout list is a JSON file `deploy/production.json` in `infra` with its own schema, and beside it lie the values of `/deployment.json`, the facts file and a self-hosted copy's own legal texts. A pull request edits it by hand, and the merge into `main` is the deployment, as long as `main` stands on this commit. The CI of the pull request checks everything without access to production, and an `api` release lower than the one rolled out is refused both by CI and by the deployment, which does only what changed. The revision of `api` gets the facts file as a volume — a version of a Secret Manager secret pinned in the revision —, and the module sets the path to it and the admission rung as environment variables.** `spec` leaves `infra` the list's format and place, who edits it, what the deployment checks and whether it refuses a lower `api`; what the list looks like and how the deployment goes step by step is in [architecture.md](architecture.md).
- **The list** — format 1, its JSON schema in `deploy/schema/`; a field the schema does not know is an error. It names:
  - the release of `api` — the tag, the name and sha256 of the binary `linux_amd64`, and the sha256 of the `Dockerfile`. It names no module packages: the build stage of `api` puts them into the image by the `modules.json` embedded in the binary;
  - the release of `app` — the tag, the name and sha256 of the build's archive, and the sha256 of `/deployment.json`;
  - the lowest rung of the admission of models of `api` — the code of a rung of `tutor`, `author` or `full` (`tutor`'s [`admission/models.go`](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/blob/28ea592/admission/models.go) at 28ea592). `infra` keeps it beside the list, and the list names it explicitly, although `api` takes `full` without a value.

  Beside it lie `deploy/production/deployment.json` — the values of `/deployment.json`, format 1 —, `deploy/production/facts.json` — the deployment facts in the form `facts` of the admission table, whose type and reading are `tutor`'s —, and, if a self-hosted copy has them, its own legal texts, `deploy/production/legal/`. The list names neither the configuration of OpenTofu, which is code of the same commit, nor the mode of reading `api_reading_mode`, which the configuration sets and `api check` checks.
- **Editing** is a pull request of `infra`, by hand. Its review is the approval of the deployment: the environment has no required approval, as in canvas-api, whose production environment has only the rule of the branch `main` (sources 8.1).
- **The checks of the pull request's CI** — without access to production:
  - the list — by its schema;
  - each release file — the binary of `api`, its `Dockerfile`, the archive of `app` — is accepted by `just -f spec/justfile release-verify <repository> <tag> <file>`, and its sha256 equals the one in the list: the recipe does not print the digest. If Sigstore or GitHub does not answer, the file is refused;
  - `deployment.json` — by the reading of the deployment configuration of the submodule's `spec`, its schema `deployment/schemas/deployment.schema.json` and its rules, and by `deployment.schema.json` from the archive of this build; a build that reads another version of the format is refused, with both named; its sha256 is the one in the list;
  - `facts.json` — by the vocabulary of facts of the admission table of the submodule's tag. This is an early sign: `api` checks the file finally, by its own version of the table, and a fact unknown to it is an error, so the deployment checks it with the image of the new release before the migrations;
  - the rung — equal to `api_admission_rung` of `tofu/production.auto.tfvars`, by default `full`; and `full` wherever `production/facts.json` names a category under 18 — `under_13`, `from_13_to_consent` or `from_consent_to_18` — for any fact of any provider: a deployment that declares facts for a learner under 18 names the rung "full" (`spec/docs/decisions.md`). The facts of adults alone pass at either rung;
  - a copy's `production/legal/` — nothing but files and folders, and `legal-check-dir` of `spec`;
  - the tag of `api` — not lower than in the list on `main`; the same one is allowed.

  CI checks the list when a pull request changes the line `api_admission_rung` of the values, though not for their other lines.
- **The deployment** — after the merge, only if `main` still stands on the commit of the list. The first job checks this, and each next one does on a retry: otherwise a rerun of an old run would roll production back, as canvas-api found ([`docs/decisions.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/decisions.md) of canvas-api). It does only what changed against the commit of the last run that deployed:
  - a new release of `api` — its tag is not lower than that of the revision that takes the traffic: `main` can be ahead of production if the previous deployment did not complete, and the pull request could have passed CI against the former `main`. Then `release-verify` and sha256 again; the image by the release's `Dockerfile` into Artifact Registry `us-east4`, by the digest from the push; the configuration check by the jobs `check` with the image of the new release, the new version of the facts and the configuration's values; the migrations job of both databases with the same image; the revision by digest — first without traffic, then with all the traffic; the job after the deployment. A change of the rung, the mode of reading, the catalog's list or the key's version in the same merge goes this way too. If the path fails after the `apply`, the configuration holds the new values while the former release serves: before a run of "facts only" by hand, which would bring them to the former image, a pull request first takes the values back or names a release that passes;
  - "facts only" — a change of the facts, the rung, the mode of reading `api_reading_mode`, the catalog's list `api_catalog_modules`, the key's version `api_anthropic_key_version`, or of a copy's `production/legal/`: a new version of the facts secret, the jobs `check` with it on the image that takes the traffic — with the copy's folder laid over it as it now is —, and only then a new revision on this image, by its digest. Without a job `check` the path refuses. No build, no migrations and no `release-verify`: the release files did not change, so removing a fact waits neither for Sigstore nor for a build. An `apply` alone makes a revision of a changed template with no traffic, so the workflow's `target` gives the part `facts` for each of these changes;
  - a new release of `app` — the release of the build on the hosting, only when the service `api` exists: a run does not roll out `app` while the commit's `production.auto.tfvars` does not set `api_serving = true` — `true` or the string `"true"`, which OpenTofu reads the same way —, and says that `app` is rolled out by hand when the service exists; the run that brought the service rolls out `app` even if it has not changed. The sign is the text of the commit's values, not the module's outputs: `target` decides before `apply`. The address of `api` in `/deployment.json` is the deterministic address of the service, the module's output `api_origin`, `https://<service>-<project number>.<region>.run.app`: it is known before the service (sources 3.9), and the file is written with the first list.

  A deployment that did not complete is repeated by rerunning its jobs, or fixed forward by a new pull request.
- **The facts and the rung.** The deployment puts `facts.json` as a new version of a Secret Manager secret — with user-managed replication, in `us-east4` — and mounts exactly this version into the revision and into the jobs `check` as a volume: Cloud Run lets one choose the version of a secret mounted as a volume (sources 3.4). The version is pinned in the revision, and a rollback of the revision reproduces it. There are no secrets in the facts, and the secret is only a way to give the revision a file. The module sets `API_ADMISSION_FACTS_FILE`, the path to the file, which does not change, and `API_ADMISSION_RUNG`, the value `api_admission_rung`, on the service and the jobs `check`; the deployment changes only the image and the version of the facts secret in the volume.
- **Rollback.** `api` — only forward, by a new release: neither CI nor the deployment lets a lower tag through. The build of `app` — by a pull request that returns its block of the list to the former values: a revert of a whole commit that also raised `api` would lower `api` and be refused. A build of `app` that has moved to a new version of the module format — only forward, and the author checks this in review. The facts, the rung, the mode of reading, the catalog and the key's version — by a pull request with the former values: a new revision on the same image. To close platform AI urgently — remove a fact by the same kind of pull request.
- **Self-hosting** keeps the same kind of `deploy/` folder with its own `deployment.json` and facts and goes the same way in its own project and its own registry: the checks and the steps of the deployment are recipes that run without GitHub Actions too. A copy on a release of `api` that does not read the mode of reading gets the pair of readings whatever the value says, and SELF-HOSTING.md names the release.

— *Why:*
- JSON with a schema — as `modules.json` of `api` and `/deployment.json` itself: the parsing is unambiguous, and the check by a schema is the same as the neighbours';
- a pull request by hand — the shared `release.yml` of `.github` has no outputs ([`release.yml`](https://github.com/altnovatrail/.github/blob/v0.1.21/.github/workflows/release.yml) of `.github` at v0.1.21): there is nothing to chain the deployment to a release with, as canvas-api did, and each release passes the author's review anyway;
- refusing an `api` release lower — both in CI and in the deployment: CI shows the error before the merge, and the deployment matches against what is actually rolled out. Otherwise a revert of the list would bring back an image of `api` with an old release of `modules`, where a withdrawn version would become current again;
- the facts file as a volume — `spec` names the facts a file of the deployment configuration of `api`, and the secret version is pinned in the revision, as a variable's value is. `spec` names the rung a value of the configuration, and a variable is enough for it;
- the variables by the module — the provider keeps the variables of a Cloud Run service as a set, and `ignore_changes` cannot leave one of them to the deployment (measurements 4.1); and the deployment has nothing to change in them;
- the rung equal to the configuration's — a list and a configuration of two rungs would check the facts on one and serve on the other;
- the rung `full` for the facts of a learner under 18, checked here — `tutor`'s list selects its entries by the deployment's rung, not by the learner's category, so at `author` the entries admitted by the author's word with no eval would read a child's work; no code of `tutor` holds this, and the check of the list is where a copy's operator learns of the condition before the facts reach `api`;
- the jobs `check` before the revision of "facts only" — facts that the check did not pass do not reach traffic, and an error in `facts.json` is not left to the revision, which would not become ready;
- `app` after the service `api` — otherwise the merge of the first list would release `app` without the service that the build calls;
- deploying what changed — removing a fact must not wait for a build, migrations and Sigstore, and a rollback of `app` must not wait for a rebuild of `api`.

— *Rejected:*
- YAML — it has ambiguities of parsing, and the neighbouring files are JSON;
- variables of OpenTofu for the list — the image, the jobs and the release of the hosting do not fit into the configuration: canvas-api kept the image outside Terraform ([`infra/terraform/service_api.tf`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/infra/terraform/service_api.tf) of canvas-api);
- a required approval of the environment — a second approval by the same author on top of the pull request's review;
- pull requests from the services' releases — the CI of `api` and `app` would need a token to write to `infra`. It can be added later, on top of this path;
- a warning instead of a refusal, and "do not check" — a revert of the list would then blindly bring back a withdrawn version of a module;
- the facts as an environment variable, JSON on one line — `spec` names the facts a file, whose type and reading are `tutor`'s, and its normative text would have to be edited;
- the facts as a file in the image — they go neither into the image nor into the git of `api`, and a change of a fact would wait for a new build;
- the variables set by the deployment, or by the module only at creation — the deployment cannot own one variable of the set, and an edit of any other variable in the module would then not reach the service;
- the rung from the list alone, read by the module from `deploy/production.json` — the configuration would depend on the list; and checking nothing — the two would hold two rungs;
- the mode of reading in the list — the list names what the deployment ships, and nothing asks for the mode there; a check of it in CI — `tofu/variables.tf` already refuses a value but `pair` and `single`;
- a merge of its own for a change of the configuration's values that a new release needs — between the two merges a release would serve values it does not hold;
- only a sentence in SELF-HOSTING.md on the rung for learners under 18 — a copy that misses it would let the entries read a child's work, and the check costs one function;
- "facts only" without the jobs `check` — an error in `facts.json` would be seen only by the revision, which would not become ready;
- an order of pull requests by hand, written in the guide, for `app` after `api` — an operator's error would release `app` without `api`; the service's address `uri` in `/deployment.json` — it exists only with the service, and the file would be completed by a second pull request;
- deploying everything on each merge — removing a fact would wait for Sigstore and migrations, and a rollback of `app` would rebuild `api`.

— *Consequence:* `deploy/production.json`; `deploy/schema/production.schema.json`; `deploy/production/`; `tools/deploy-check/`; `tools/deploy/api.sh`; `tools/deploy/target.mjs`; `tools/deploy/test/`; `tofu/api.tf`; `tofu/outputs.tf`; `tofu/variables.tf`; `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`; `.claude/rules/deploy.md`; `docs/architecture.md`; SELF-HOSTING.md.

## Hosting and domains

**R06. The build of `app` is served by Firebase Hosting — the default site of the platform's project. The first deployment has no domain of its own: `app` lives at the project's `web.app` address, and `api` at its own Cloud Run address `*.run.app`, with CORS for the exact origin of `app`. The main domain and the widgets' domain are bought together after О-16, before the public launch (Т-17).** How it is built is in [architecture.md](architecture.md).
- **Hosting** — Firebase Hosting, the project's default site, the Blaze plan: on the free Spark the site is turned off when a quota is exceeded, and the project has billing anyway. Storage up to 10 GB and transfer up to 10 GB a month are free (sources 4.1). Blaze has no spending cap, so the module sets the project's budget with an alert; the budget takes off the charges the credit of the free tier alone, `FREE_TIER`, and no other credit: `credit_types_treatment = "INCLUDE_SPECIFIED_CREDITS"` (sources 7.3; measurements 4.1).
- **The release of the build** is the step of the deployment of `app`. The rollout assembles the release folder: the archive of the new build, the `assets/` of the build that is now released on Hosting, and `/deployment.json` from `deploy/production/deployment.json`. Then it writes the version's configuration and releases everything as one version of Hosting: "The content and configuration served by each channel is packaged into a version object" (sources 4.1).
  - **The previous build** is the one that is released now, not the one named in the list on `main`: `main` can be ahead of production if the previous deployment did not complete. The rollout learns it from the message of the release of the site's live channel, `app <tag> <archive> <sha256>`, and after `keeping` the same about the build whose `assets/` this release kept. If the list names the very build that is released, the previous one is the one whose `assets/` its release kept: a repeated release of the same build keeps what the first one kept. No release means the first release, and the folder is only the new build. Another message means the release was not made by the rollout, and the rollout refuses: there is nothing to check the files of such a build with in order to keep them further. The archive of the previous build passes the same checks as the new one, `release-verify` and sha256: only a checked file gets into the job that reaches production.
  - The rollout puts in the files of the previous build itself: a version is a full set of files, and Т-17 says to keep the files of the previous release. canvas-api did not do this, and its next release removed the files of the previous one ([`justfile`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/justfile) of canvas-api, `ci-hosting-check`).
  - `headers`: on all paths — `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff` and HSTS; on all paths outside `/assets/**` — the CSP by the archive's `csp.json`, with `{api}` and `{authDomain}` from `deployment.json` and with `frame-ancestors 'none'`, and `Cache-Control: no-cache`; on `/assets/**` — `immutable`. Not by the path `index.html`: Hosting matches headers against the path of the request before rewrites (sources 4.1), and an SPA path rewritten to `index.html` would otherwise be left without the CSP and without `no-cache`;
  - the SPA rewrite to `index.html` — only outside `/assets/**`, by `!/@(assets)/**`, which production accepts though RE2 has no lookahead (sources 4.4): Hosting rewrites a path only if there is no file at it (sources 4.1), and without the exception it would serve `index.html` for a request for a file that is gone; `/deployment.json` is in every version;
  - `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin` is not sent: it breaks the Firebase sign-in window.

  After the release the rollout checks the responses: the bytes of `index.html`, the headers on `/`, on an SPA path and on a file in `/assets/`, a `404` for an unknown file in `/assets/`, as `ci-hosting-check` of canvas-api does ([`justfile`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/justfile) of canvas-api).
- **Rollback** — a pull request that restores the earlier block `app` of the list. A rollback by a version in the Firebase console is not used: the list stays the truth.
- **Logs.** Firebase Hosting itself keeps IP addresses — "Hosting retains IP data for a few months" —, this is not configurable, and the service is global: the platform's region does not hold it (sources 4.1). Do not turn on the export of Hosting's logs to Cloud Logging: it is optional, and its entries have `remoteIp` (sources 4.1). Google is a processor of data for Hosting.
- **Addresses:**
  - the first deployment has no domain of its own: `app` — `<project>.web.app`, `api` — `*.run.app`. So it was in canvas-api too: «свой домен не нужен закрытой бете по приглашениям, а domain mapping Cloud Run в Preview» ([`docs/decisions.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/decisions.md) of canvas-api);
  - the default site answers at two addresses, `web.app` and `firebaseapp.com`, with the same content (sources 4.1). The origin of `app` is only `web.app`: it is named by the CORS of `api` and by `/deployment.json`, and `app` from `firebaseapp.com` will not reach `api`. The project's `firebaseapp.com` is the `authDomain` of Firebase Auth: `app` signs in by a window, the sign-in page is `/__/auth/handler` on it, and there is no need to keep it on `web.app`;
  - the module sets the authorized domains of the Firebase project, the whole list: `<project>.firebaseapp.com` and `<project>.web.app`. Firebase itself puts the same two addresses there, and `localhost`, which it put for projects created before 28 April 2025, the module removes (sources 4.2). The rollout of `app` does not touch them;
  - when a domain of its own appears, Hosting gets it, and how `api` gets it is decided at the same time: Cloud Run domain mapping is in Preview (sources 1.4). For the widgets' domain of stage 4 `infra` prepares nothing now: where the widgets' bundles are hosted is Т-17.
- **The samples page of `canvas`**, when it moves to Firebase Hosting, moves as a separate site of the project with its own origin: "Erase everything on this device" and another account's sign-in in `app` erase the web storage of its origin, and Hosting keeps several sites in one project (sources 4.1). Children's samples are made with it too, so the IP addresses of the devices on which they are made will then also get into Firebase's logs for the same period.
- **Self-hosting** — any hosting that sends its own headers, by the same steps; without its own headers — weaker protection.

— *Why:*
- of the options that send their own headers and release the files with the headers as one version, Firebase Hosting alone requires neither code of its own nor a domain of its own, and it is already in the same project as Firebase Auth;
- a closed slice needs no domain of its own, and О-16 says to check the trademark before registering a domain;
- different origins — the requests to `api` do not go through the hosting, and its logs do not see each request of a learner; `api` holds CORS for the exact origin of `app`;
- the authorized domains by the module — the right to change them, `firebaseauth.configs.update`, is held by the configuration's service account anyway, while the rollout would have to be given it anew, and it cannot be narrowed to the domains (sources 4.6);
- the previous build by the release's message — a Hosting release has a `message` of up to 512 characters, and `channels/live` gives it together with the release, while the Hosting API does not let one download the files of the released version (sources 4.5);
- the budget with the free tier's credit alone — the Free Trial's credit, a `PROMOTION`, would make every charge of the project zero to the budget, and the alert at 50, 90 and 100 % would come only once the credit ran out; after the upgrade the credit left stays until the 90 days of the trial are over, so an upgrade alone would not let the budget see the charges either (sources 7.3).

— *Rejected:*
- Cloud Storage with an external load balancer and Cloud CDN — for a backend bucket the load balancer's log "is automatically enabled and cannot be disabled", a certificate needs a domain of its own, and there is no release as one version (sources 4.3);
- Cloud Run with a static server — it is a service with its own code and release, and `infra` holds no code of services. For static content Google names Firebase Hosting and Cloud Storage (sources 4.3);
- Cloudflare Pages — one more processor outside Google, no retention period of IP addresses was found in its terms, and without a `404.html` it serves `index.html` for any path (sources 4.3);
- GitHub Pages — sends no headers of its own: "We don't support this feature today so a `meta` tag unfortunately is the only way" (sources 4.3);
- a domain of its own for the first deployment — against О-16;
- one origin through a Hosting rewrite into Cloud Run — each request to `api` would get into the hosting's logs, and the timeout of Hosting is 60 seconds (sources 4.1), and whether it buffers responses is not checked;
- the previous build by the list on `main` — `main` can be ahead of production, and then the files of a build that never went out would go into the version, and the files of the released one would be lost;
- the authorized domains by the rollout — it would need a right that cannot be narrowed to them;
- `EXCLUDE_ALL_CREDITS` for the budget — it would count the free tier as spending and alert on usage that costs nothing; the budget's default, all credits — the alert would see nothing until the trial's credit is gone.

— *Consequence:* `tofu/firebase.tf`; `tofu/sign_in.tf`; `tofu/budget.tf`; `tofu/identities.tf`; `tools/deploy/app.mjs`; `docs/architecture.md`; SELF-HOSTING.md.

## Logs and traces

**R07. The request logs of Cloud Run are all excluded — by the filter `LOG_ID("run.googleapis.com/requests")` in the sink `_Default` and in every sink the project sets up —, and so are Data Access audit but the reads of Secret Manager's secret versions, the activity log of Identity Platform and the request logs of Firebase Hosting; the reads of the secret versions are audited and kept. For the analysis of failures the log of `api`, the built-in metrics of Cloud Run and the alerts on them remain. The platform writes no traces of its own; the Cloud Trace API stays on, since Cloud Scheduler depends on it, and the traces Cloud Run writes itself go to `_Trace` in `us-east4`. The platform's logs are kept for 30 days in a bucket in `us-east4`, and Google Cloud's audit in `_Required` for 400 days in `global`, a period that is not configurable.** How it is built is in [architecture.md](architecture.md).
- **The exclusion.** The filter stands in the sink `_Default`, which is directed into the log bucket in `us-east4`: an entry goes to the sink's destination only if it "matches the sink's inclusion filter but doesn't match any of the sink's exclusion filters" (sources 2.1). The same filters are in every sink the project sets up. `_Required` does not get request logs, and its sink does not change. The name of the log in the filter is the one the entries come under: the page of Cloud Run shows it only in an example entry, `run.googleapis.com%2Frequests` (sources 2.3), and on the project, while `api` took requests, neither the bucket nor `_Required` held one entry of that log or with `httpRequest` — the bucket only `run.googleapis.com/stdout`, `stderr` and `varlog/system`.
- **What the Logging API takes in.** The filter applies to an entry Google has already received: "Exclusion filters are applied after log entries are received by the Logging API or Telemetry API" (sources 2.1). The IP address reaches Google as a processor, but is not kept in the bucket, and such an entry is not charged (sources 2.3). The platform's promise — no direct identifiers and IP addresses in its logs — is about the logs that are kept.
- **The other exclusions.** Each sink also excludes these, so that they stay out of the bucket even if someone else turns them on — the organization of a self-hosted copy, whose inherited audit config the project does not cancel, a button in a console, or a future edit:
  - Data Access audit, `LOG_ID("cloudaudit.googleapis.com/data_access") AND NOT (protoPayload.serviceName="secretmanager.googleapis.com" AND (protoPayload.methodName:"AccessSecretVersion" OR protoPayload.methodName:"GetSecretVersion"))`: it is off by default and holds `callerIp` (sources 2.3);
  - the activity log of Identity Platform, `LOG_ID("identitytoolkit.googleapis.com/requests")`: "Activity logging is disabled by default", and it holds each sign-in request (sources 2.3);
  - the request logs of Firebase Hosting, by the resource type, `resource.type="firebase_domain"`: their export to Cloud Logging is turned on only by a button in the Firebase console, which the module does not see, the entries have `remoteIp`, and Firebase names the log `webrequests` and the resource type but gives no full `LOG_ID` (sources 4.6).
- **The reads of the secrets.** The module turns on Data Access, `DATA_READ`, for `secretmanager.googleapis.com` alone — `google_project_iam_audit_config.secret_reads`, authoritative for that one service and touching no other ([`google_project_iam_audit_config`](https://github.com/hashicorp/terraform-provider-google/blob/v8.10.0/website/docs/r/google_project_iam.html.markdown) at v8.10.0): the Data Access audit of the other services stays as Google's default or a copy's organization leaves it, and is excluded. Only `DATA_READ`: the writes of Secret Manager are Admin Activity, always on; no caller is exempted. The exclusion lets through the two version reads by their method names, not the whole service: a list or a policy read of Secret Manager, which a copy's organization may turn on beside them, stays excluded. The audit config is named under `roles/resourcemanager.projectIamAdmin` of `infra-config` in `tofu/bootstrap.sh`: its condition counts the role bindings a request modifies, and an audit config modifies none (sources 2.6); the apply writes it under the condition.
  - **What an entry holds.** The reader, `callerIp`, the secret's name and the request's metadata, and no value; about 1.8 KB. Even 10 000 reads a day would be some 0.5 GiB a month, about 1% of the free 50 GiB of logs a project a month (sources 7.1), so the cost moves nothing at any count the service, its jobs and CI can make.
  - **The address.** The readers are `api-run`, the deployment's and CI's accounts, the eval jobs of `modules` and `tutor` through the pool `github-eval`, which read the eval keys from GitHub's runners, and the operator: the address is Google's, a GitHub runner's or the operator's, not a learner's. On the project every read is `api-run`'s, `AccessSecretVersion` from the address `private`, of the service's secrets, `api-anthropic-key` among them, and `api-deploy` and `infra-config` deployed and applied without reading one; what an entry of another reader holds is Д29. The platform's description of its logs names this audit — the bucket in `us-east4`, 30 days, the reader's email and IP address, no entry of an account owner or a learner. Whoever may change the sink or the audit config — `infra-config` — may also hide its own reads; such a change is Admin Activity, `UpdateSink` or `SetIamPolicy` with `auditConfigs`, in `_Required`.
- **The other logs:**
  - the logs of the container of `api` and of its jobs — the events of `api` without identifiers and addresses ([`api` v0.1.22](https://github.com/altnovatrail/api/blob/v0.1.22/internal/logging/logging.go)) —, and the system logs of Cloud Run: 30 days in the bucket in `us-east4`. Jobs have no request logs: "Request logs (services only)" (sources 2.3);
  - Cloud Scheduler's log of its attempts, `cloudscheduler.googleapis.com/executions`: `_Default` routes it into the bucket in `us-east4`, for 30 days. Its entries `AttemptStarted` and `AttemptFinished` hold the job, its target, the scheduled time, Google's text `debugInfo` and the HTTP status, and neither an address nor a person's email, so no sink excludes it. Google's pages name the two types, but neither the log nor its fields (sources 2.5);
  - Admin Activity — always, in `_Required`, 400 days, in `global` (Д3): "you can't configure, exclude, or disable them", and an entry holds `callerIp` and the caller's email (sources 2.3). It is written by the operations that change the project's resources and settings, which the operator and CI make. `SignUp` of Identity Platform — the creation of a user — writes Admin Activity too, while Google sign-in, `SignInWithIdp`, produces no audit logs (sources 2.3): nothing in the project calls `SignUp`, the learners' users come by the Google window, and the entries of Identity Platform in Admin Activity are those of the setup of sign-in, with `callerIp`, `callerSuppliedUserAgent` and `principalEmail` of whoever made it. A new call of `api` to Identity Toolkit is checked again before its release;
  - System Event — also in `_Required`: Google's own actions on the project's resources, among them each run of a job, `/Jobs.RunJob`, which names no service account of the project. The run of a job is Data Access by Google's list (sources 2.5), which is off and excluded;
  - Policy Denied goes to `_Default` and is kept for 30 days: it holds the one whom a policy denied, and their `callerIp` (sources 2.3);
  - Firebase Auth and Firebase Hosting keep IP addresses themselves (sources 1.7).
- **For the analysis of failures** — the log of `api`: the Connect interceptor writes the method, the code, the reason of the refusal and the time, and the jobs write counters, without identifiers ([`api` 5a02ff4](https://github.com/altnovatrail/api/blob/5a02ff4/docs/architecture.md); [`internal/logging/events.go`](https://github.com/altnovatrail/api/blob/v0.1.22/internal/logging/events.go) at v0.1.22); the built-in metrics of Cloud Run — the number of requests, latencies, instances —, which Cloud Monitoring collects itself and free of charge; Error Reporting — by the logs of `api` that are kept (sources 2.3). The project sets up no log-based metrics of its own with labels from `httpRequest` or others where there would be an address: "User-defined log-based metrics are calculated from both included and excluded logs" (sources 2.3).
- **The alerts** are on the built-in metrics, by email to the operator, and only when the operator is named. Two of them watch the jobs of `api` with a `schedule`, by `run.googleapis.com/job/completed_execution_count` (sources 3.8), one metric in each condition:
  - **a failed execution** — the executions with the label `result` not `succeeded`. `sweep` exits with code 1 when a pass is not clean, and the next one picks up what is left (sources 3.8; [`api` v0.1.14](https://github.com/altnovatrail/api/blob/v0.1.14/README.md)), but a pass that failed day after day would leave the deletions and the retention periods standing, and no one would learn of it. On the project the label is `succeeded` and `failed`; the condition is "not `succeeded`", not "`failed`": a value written otherwise gives extra emails, not silence. An execution that did not start writes no point, and this alert does not see it;
  - **a job by the clock that has not run** — every `result`, summed over the last 25 hours, each job in a condition of its own, the condition met when the sum is under one or there are no data at all, mailed when the incident opens and only then. Cloud Scheduler has no metric of its own and retries no failed call (sources 2.5); the count of executions sees a day with no run whatever its cause — the rights of `api-scheduler`, the job's address, a paused schedule, Cloud Scheduler itself — and reads no log. A job writes no point on a day with no execution, so taking no data as met makes a threshold condition behave as one on absence, and 25 hours, 90 000 seconds, is the longest window a condition may roll (sources 3.14). A run at 09:00 writes its point about a minute later; with a daily run under the hour the window always holds one, and a missed day empties it about an hour after the run it missed. That the missing data are those of the window's sum, not of the raw points, the pages do not say, and the project shows it: the window did not open between two runs, through a morning when the last point was nearly 24 hours old (measurements 3.2). A run longer than an hour, or a schedule rarer than daily, would empty the window with nothing missed, so a job by the clock runs every day and may take 3000s at most: the module refuses another schedule or timeout. It mails at the opening alone: Cloud Monitoring also closes an incident that has had no data for a day more than `auto_close` (sources 3.14), and a mail of that close would read as a run. A condition for each job keeps one job's points from standing for another's missing ones; a job made, renamed or added has no data until its first run, so the alert may mail before it, and the mail says so;
  - **the price** — $0.35 a month for each metric in a condition from September 1, 2027, and $0.50 a million points its query returns (sources 5.4).
- **Traces.** The platform writes none of its own. The Cloud Trace API is on: Google turns it on when the project is created, and `gcloud services disable cloudtrace.googleapis.com` refuses — "FAILED_PRECONDITION: The service cloudtrace.googleapis.com is depended on by the following active service(s): cloudapis.googleapis.com,cloudscheduler.googleapis.com". Cloud Run itself traces a part of the incoming requests — "Incoming requests to Cloud Run services automatically generate traces" —, and the sampling is not configurable (sources 2.3). Such traces go to `_Trace` in `us-east4`, whose place is set before the first trace, and their attributes are matched against the platform's promise for its logs with the first one (Д17); `traces.list` needs the header `x-goog-user-project` for a user's sign-in. Traces of `api` come later, by a decision of their own together with `api`.
- **Periods.** The log bucket in `us-east4` — 30 days: the period is set from 1 to 3650 days. `_Required` — 400 days, "Not configurable" (sources 2.3); its entries are the operator's and CI's, not an account owner's or a learner's.

— *Why:*
- a field cannot be cut out of a Cloud Logging entry: rights on a field only hide it — "Field-level access controls let you hide individual LogEntry fields from users" —, and it goes on being kept (sources 2.3). So an entry with an address is either kept whole or not kept at all;
- the code and the method of a request are in the log of `api` and in the built-in metrics anyway, and the address is not needed for the analysis of failures;
- the exclusions of what the module does not turn on — Google's default holds only until someone else turns a log on, and each of these holds a person's address or each of their sign-ins;
- the reads of the secrets kept — in a breach the operator has to know whether a connection string to `api`'s databases was read, and by whom; an entry cannot be kept without its address, here the address is no learner's, and the entries hold what a breach needs: who read which secret, and when;
- no traces of the platform's own — the attributes of the automatic traces are not described, and the log and the metrics are enough;
- 30 days is the default period and the period of canvas-api ([`docs/decisions.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/decisions.md) of canvas-api); the platform's logs have neither identifiers nor addresses, and one author needs a margin for the analysis of failures.

— *Rejected:*
- keeping the request logs with a 5xx code — the address would stay in the kept entry;
- keeping request logs briefly instead of excluding them, as canvas-api recommends ([`docs/privacy.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/privacy.md) of canvas-api) — the address is kept all the same;
- relying on Google's default for Data Access, the activity log of Identity Platform and the export of Hosting's logs — someone else can turn them on;
- turning the reads of the secrets on by hand for a check — the project would differ from the module, and the exclusion lifted by hand would let through every Data Access entry, not Secret Manager's alone, until an apply restored it; not logging them without seeing an entry;
- a metric of its own by request logs — the code and the method are already in the built-in metrics and in the log of `api`, and a label from `httpRequest` would carry the address out of an excluded entry;
- no alert on a failed execution, the job's log alone — a failure would be seen only by whoever looks;
- for a job by the clock that has not run: a condition on absence, `condition_absent` — it waits 23.5 hours at most (sources 3.14), less than a day between two runs, so it would open at each day's gap; a log-match alert on the entries `AttemptFinished` of Cloud Scheduler's log — it sees a call that failed, but not a paused schedule or a Cloud Scheduler that did not call, and what a failed attempt writes is not seen; a log-based metric of that log — the same blind spot, and a metric of the project's own; both alerts — more code for what the metric already sees;
- removing Cloud Scheduler and turning the Trace API off by force, `--force`, with everything that depends on it — the scheduled jobs would have to be started otherwise, and `cloudapis` would drag along what else depends on it;
- traces of `api` through OpenTelemetry now — they need a trace identifier in the events table of `api` and a decision of `api`, and the log and the metrics are enough;
- 7 days — a failure noticed after a week can no longer be analysed; 90 days — longer than needed, and there is nothing personal in the platform's logs anyway.

— *Not checked:* what Cloud Scheduler writes for a failed attempt — none has failed; that a missed day opens the alert on a job that has not run — no run was missed, and a day's pause would hold back the deletions `sweep` finishes (Д21); the attributes of the traces Cloud Run writes itself (Д17); an entry of a read of a secret by the operator, by CI or by an eval job (Д29).

— *Consequence:* `tofu/logging.tf`; `tofu/alerts.tf`; `tofu/apis.tf`; `tofu/sign_in.tf`; `tofu/variables.tf`; `tofu/bootstrap.sh`; `tofu/tests/logging.tftest.hcl`; `tofu/tests/budget_alerts.tftest.hcl`; `tofu/tests/api.tftest.hcl`; `docs/architecture.md`; BREACH.md.

— *Consequence:* `tofu/`; `deploy/`; `tools/deploy/`; `.devcontainer/Dockerfile`; CLAUDE.md; `docs/architecture.md`.

## The container

**R09. The container of `infra` differs from the container of `spec` by three tools in the stage `toolchain` — OpenTofu, ShellCheck and actionlint —, each at the exact version of an `ARG` with the sha256 sums of its archives for each architecture. The stage is `FROM toolchain-core`, the template's stage of Node, just, Go and buf, and installs the three by a `RUN` of its own. OpenTofu's MPL 2.0 and ShellCheck's GPL-3.0 are named exceptions of the license; actionlint's MIT is compatible with Apache-2.0. At installation an archive is checked only against its sum, and the signature of OpenTofu's file of sums is checked by hand with `gpg` at each raise of its version.** A move to a new tag of `spec` keeps this difference when it matches `.devcontainer/` against the template: the stage's lines stay as they are.
- **The stage `toolchain`:**
  - `ARG OPENTOFU_VERSION`, `ARG SHELLCHECK_VERSION` and `ARG ACTIONLINT_VERSION` in `.devcontainer/Dockerfile`, each with its `_SHA256_AMD64` and `_SHA256_ARM64`;
  - the sums: OpenTofu's from the release's `tofu_<version>_SHA256SUMS`, actionlint's from `actionlint_<version>_checksums.txt` ([v1.7.12](https://github.com/rhysd/actionlint/releases/tag/v1.7.12)), and ShellCheck's, which publishes no list of sums, the field `digest` of the release's assets in GitHub's API, matched against `sha256sum` of both downloaded archives (measurements 4.2). Each is checked by the same `fetch` as the core's archives, before unpacking;
  - only the binary is taken from each archive, into `/usr/local/bin`, and the tool prints its version at the end of its step;
  - a version is raised by an edit of its `ARG` together with both sums; Dependabot's `docker` does not see it.
- **The signature.** OpenTofu's `tofu_<version>_SHA256SUMS.gpgsig` is checked with the key [opentofu.asc](https://get.opentofu.org/opentofu.asc), fingerprint `E3E6 E43D 84CB 852E ADB0 051D 0C0A F313 E5FD 9F80` (measurements 4.2), at each raise of the version, before the new sums go into the `ARG`s: first the fingerprint of the downloaded key is matched against this one, and a different fingerprint stops the raise and becomes a question to the author — a key that came from the same site as the sums proves nothing by itself.
- **The licenses.** OpenTofu is under MPL 2.0 (sources 5.2), ShellCheck under "GNU General Public License, version 3" (sources 5.3). Both are development and CI tools: they ship nowhere and put none of their own code into what ships, no archive or image of NovaTrail holds them, and the platform can be built, run and deployed without them. The shared rule on licenses allows such a tool another OSI-approved license only as a named exception in the decision that brings it in: this entry is that exception for both. actionlint is under "the MIT License" (sources 5.5) and goes into no release.
- **The CI checks.** The job `OpenTofu` of `ci.yml` runs in the stage `toolchain`, through `spec/tools/toolchain/toolchain.sh`, the recipes of the three tools beside the module's checks: `ci-tofu-test` and `ci-tofu` — `tofu version -json` names the version of the `ARG` —, `ci-shellcheck-test` and `ci-shellcheck` — `shellcheck --version` names its version, and `tofu/bootstrap.sh` passes with no findings —, and `ci-actionlint-test` and `ci-actionlint` — actionlint over `.github/workflows`, with the stage's ShellCheck. In the devcontainer a recipe catches a container not rebuilt after a raise of an `ARG`. The check `OpenTofu` is required in the ruleset of `main`. The shared Documents check and the job `Deploy` run in the core, which holds none of the three.

— *Why:*
- the checks of `infra`'s code need the tools, and they run in the stage `toolchain`, so the tools are there and not in `devcontainer`, which CI does not build. OpenTofu costs about 35 MB more of download at each build of the stage;
- the sum in the `ARG` already pins the archive's bytes, as for the core's tools, and the signature confirms that OpenTofu issued these sums, which is needed once, when the sums are read;
- ShellCheck — `bootstrap.sh` hands out rights once in a project with billing, and it is worth checking not only by hand, as canvas-api did;
- actionlint — the deploy workflow runs only after a merge, and an error in it would otherwise first show in production; like ShellCheck, the checking tool stands where the author checks too.

— *Rejected:*
- checking the signature with `gpg` in the Dockerfile, with a key pinned by its fingerprint: a step no other tool has, and two more downloads at each build, while the sum pins the archive's bytes anyway;
- the stage `devcontainer`: CI does not build it, and the check would not find the tools there;
- installing the tools on the runner by an action, as canvas-api installed Terraform: locally and in CI there would be different installations, not one stage;
- no ShellCheck, only `bash -n` and the test on a stub `gcloud`: the test catches an error of quoting or an empty variable only if its scenario touched it;
- the image `rhysd/actionlint` by digest, as canvas-api had it: the devcontainer has no Docker, and the author could not check a workflow themselves.

— *Consequence:* `.devcontainer/Dockerfile`; `justfile`; `.github/workflows/ci.yml`; `.claude/rules/ci.md`; CLAUDE.md; `docs/architecture.md`.

## The root module

**R10. The OpenTofu root module lies in `tofu/`: OpenTofu and the providers `hashicorp/google` and `hashicorp/google-beta` at exact versions, the lock file from the OpenTofu registry, with the hashes of every platform the providers are released for, and the backend a partial `gcs`. The tests run on mock providers, without the cloud, with every variable of the module set in `tofu/tests/terraform.tfvars`, and a run of them lists every resource, data source and output of the module. Dependabot raises the providers, ecosystem `opentofu`, in one group.** The model is [`infra/terraform/`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/tree/0901af0/infra/terraform) of canvas-api.
- **The files:**
  - `versions.tf` — `required_version` the same as `ARG OPENTOFU_VERSION` of the container: a raise of the `ARG` without this line stops `tofu init`, so the two are kept together. The providers `google` and `google-beta` are at one version: the Firebase resources are only in `google-beta`, and the two come out together. The providers are configured with `billing_project` and `user_project_override`, as canvas-api had them: otherwise the budget API and the Firebase API refuse whoever signs in as a person;

  - `apis.tf` — the APIs of the module's parts, each with a comment on which part takes it, and none is turned off on removal; the Cloud Trace API is not in the list;
  - `tests/configuration.tftest.hcl` — `mock_provider` and `command = plan` ([`tofu test`](https://github.com/opentofu/opentofu/blob/v1.13.9/website/docs/cli/commands/test/index.mdx) at v1.13.9): the list of APIs is exactly that of `apis.tf`, without Cloud Trace, and `project_id` and `region` are accepted and refused at the edges of their forms;
  - the recipes `tf-fmt` and `tf-check` — the format, `init -backend=false -lockfile=readonly`, `validate`, `test` and the checks of the module's lists; `tf-check` runs in the CI job `OpenTofu`, required in the ruleset of `main`.
- **The lock file.** `tofu providers lock` from the registry `registry.opentofu.org` writes the hashes `h1:` and `zh:` of all the platforms of a provider's release, and `-platform` does not narrow this: with a single `-platform=linux_amd64` the file is the same, byte for byte (measurements 4.3). So a container on amd64 and on arm64 installs the providers by one file. `init` with `-lockfile=readonly` refuses a provider the lock file does not name.
- **Dependabot.** The ecosystem `opentofu`, the directory `/tofu`, once a week, a cooldown of 3 days, the group `google-providers`. When it raises a version it writes the provider's block of the lock file anew from the registry's hashes — `h1:` for the platforms whose `h1:` hashes are already in the file and the new release ships, here all of them, and `zh:` for every platform —, and only without them by `tofu providers lock` for those platforms ([`file_updater.rb`](https://github.com/dependabot/dependabot-core/blob/65e460a4a956b8a71bc27ddab3b97151b8abe289/opentofu/lib/dependabot/opentofu/file_updater.rb) of dependabot-core at 65e460a). OpenTofu itself it does not see: it is raised by hand, with the container's `ARG`.
- **The licenses.** `terraform-provider-google` and `terraform-provider-google-beta` are under MPL 2.0 (sources 5.2). The providers are part of a tool of development and deployment: they ship nowhere and put none of their own code into what ships. The shared rule on licenses allows such a tool another OSI-approved license only as a named exception in the decision that brings it in: this entry is that exception.
- **The test values.** `tofu test` also reads `tofu/production.auto.tfvars`, so `tofu/tests/terraform.tfvars` sets every variable of the module: a value the platform or a copy writes there — `api_image`, `api_serving`, its own environments — would otherwise reach the tests and fail them, and the required check would hold the merge. A variable with a default is set there not to a copy of the default but to `null`, and the tests read the default itself: a copy would let a typo in the default pass, since no test read it, and CI of a pull request has no plan. Such a variable has `nullable = false`, for which OpenTofu takes the default for `null`, or the default `null`: for a variable that allows `null`, `null` would stay `null`, not the default. In a run's assertion `var.<name>` stays the `null` of the file, so a run that checks a default reads the module's output instead; overriding a variable in a run works as usual. `_tf-test-values` refuses a variable the file leaves unset and one with a default set to anything but `null`.
- **The copy's list.** Every resource, data source and output of the module is in the list of the run `nothing_of_the_platform_reaches_a_copy` of `tofu/tests/copy.tftest.hcl`, the list names nothing the module lacks, and `_tf-copy` refuses otherwise: the list is written by hand and has fallen behind the module. The run holds that the platform's project, region and numbers reach none of a copy's resources and calls. `_tf-copy` compares two sets, as `_tf-roles` does: each `resource`, `data` and `output` declared in `tofu/*.tf`, and the names on the run's lines that start with `jsonencode(`, so that a commented entry or a name in a message does not count.

— *Why:* the tests on mock providers run in CI without signing in to the cloud, as at canvas-api. The whole repository is infrastructure, so the directory `tofu/` is named by what is in it, beside `deploy/`. canvas-api raised its providers by hand because Dependabot would have pinned them by its own version of Terraform, while OpenTofu has its own ecosystem, which keeps the platforms of the lock file.

— *Rejected:*
- `infra/terraform/`, as at canvas-api: the name would repeat the repository's name and name the wrong tool; the module at the repository's root — `tofu fmt -recursive` would go through `spec/` too;
- the providers by hand, by a recipe `tf-lock`: a raise is easy to forget, and Dependabot does the same;
- OpenTofu's state encryption: the state holds no secrets' values — the operator or the deployment puts them, not the module —, the GCS bucket encrypts it itself, and the encryption key would be one more secret without which the state cannot be read;
- enabling each API in the file of the part that takes it first, as canvas-api did: the list is kept in one place and by one test;
- for the test values: removing the variables with a default from the test file — their value for the tests would come from `production.auto.tfvars`; a test directory of its own without that file — `tofu test` reads the module's `production.auto.tfvars` anyway, and the mock providers would have to be repeated; a check in `just` that the default equals the copy — parsing HCL as text, and the tests would still read the copy;
- for the copy's list: building it in the test from the state — `tofu test` gives an assertion no list of the resources it made; looking for each name anywhere in the run as a substring — `api_jobs` is found inside `api_jobs_missed`, and a commented entry passes, the very omissions the check is for.

— *Consequence:* `tofu/`; `justfile`; `.github/workflows/ci.yml`; `.github/dependabot.yml`; `.gitignore`; `.claude/rules/tofu.md`; `docs/architecture.md`.

## Cloud Run `api`

**R11. The module describes the service and the jobs of `api`: it creates the jobs when `api_image` names the first image from its own registry, and the service after them, when `api_serving` says so too; from then on the deployment changes the image and the version of the facts secret in the volume, and the module does not touch them. The jobs are the list `api_jobs`, which holds only what the binary of `api` gives — `check`, `migrate`, `after` and `sweep` by default —, each placed in the deployment by its field `deploy`; a Cloud Scheduler schedule belongs to the jobs it is named for. `api` and the deployment have service accounts of their own with rights on their own resources, and the deployment gets in through the Workload Identity Federation pool only from the GitHub environment `production`. A request to the service may run as long as `api`'s own write timeout.** The model is [`service_api.tf`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/infra/terraform/service_api.tf) and [`iam.tf`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/infra/terraform/iam.tf) of canvas-api.
- **The first image.** A Cloud Run resource cannot be created without an image, and the image of `api` appears in `images` only with the first deployment. While `api_image` is not set, there is neither the service, nor the jobs, nor the rights on them, but the service accounts and the deployment's binding to the pool exist: the first deployment can put the image. The check of `api_image` lets through only a digest from `<region>-docker.pkg.dev/<project>/images/`.
- **First the jobs, then the service.** `api_image` creates the jobs, and the service appears only when `api_serving = true` as well. So the first deployment keeps the deployment's order, the check and the migrations before a revision takes requests: a pull request with `api_image` — the jobs exist — the deployment runs the check of the configuration and `migrate` — a pull request with `api_serving = true` — the service comes up on a database already checked. Otherwise the first revision would take public traffic before the migrations and before the check of the facts.
- **The boundary with the deployment.** The module owns the form of the service and the jobs: the scaling, the resources, `GET /health` as the startup probe, the service account, the variables and the volumes, and the container's name, `api`, of the service and of every job. The deployment keeps the image of the service and of each job and the version of the facts secret in the volume: the module sets them at creation — the image `api_image`, the version `latest` — and does not touch them, by `ignore_changes`. The facts are the first volume, and the deployment finds it by its path with `gcloud`'s `--update-secrets` and edits the module's volume in place: `gcloud` tells its own container from the others by name, and in an unnamed one it makes a volume of its own and leaves the module's unmounted (`SecretVolumeChanges.Adjust`). On `novatrail` the service and the job `check` have the one volume, the module's, with the deployment's version, and an `apply` after a deployment changes neither. The module does not describe the traffic, as at canvas-api: an `apply` that changes the service's template makes a revision with no traffic, since the deployment has given all of it to one revision by name, and the change reaches the traffic with the next deployment of `api` — `release` or `facts` —, whose revision is made from the template; a job gets it at its next execution (sources 3.13). The provider sends the service whole by `PATCH`, with the traffic the state holds when the configuration has no block `traffic` ([`resource_cloud_run_v2_service.go`](https://github.com/hashicorp/terraform-provider-google/blob/v8.10.0/google/services/cloudrunv2/resource_cloud_run_v2_service.go) at v8.10.0).
- **The service's variables and volumes:**
  - the facts: the secret `api-admission-facts` as the first volume, the file `/etc/api/admission/facts.json` in `API_ADMISSION_FACTS_FILE`; the lowest rung, `API_ADMISSION_RUNG`, from `api_admission_rung`, `full` by default; the mode of reading handwriting, `API_READING_MODE`, from `api_reading_mode`, `pair` or `single`, `pair` by default and set always, as the rung is; and `API_CATALOG_MODULES`, the IDs by commas, when `api_catalog_modules` names modules;
  - the platform's key of Anthropic as the second volume: the secret `api-anthropic-key` at the exact version `api_anthropic_key_version`, the file `/etc/api/anthropic/key` in `API_ANTHROPIC_KEY_FILE`. The key's version is the configuration's, which a deployment does not change; the module makes the secret without a value on every copy and never deletes it;
  - the pooler's strings of both databases, `API_DATABASE_URL` and `API_OPENFGA_DATABASE_URL`, from the latest version of the secrets `api-database-url` and `api-openfga-database-url`, as variables, not volumes: `api` takes a secret from either alike, and more volumes would let `--update-secrets` reorder them, and the next `apply` give the facts `latest` back. The module creates these secrets before the service, with `deletion_protection = true`; their values are put before `api_serving = true`;
  - `API_APP_ORIGIN`, the origin of the default Hosting site, `https://<site>.web.app`, whose CORS `api` answers;
  - `API_CONSENT_FORMS_BUCKET`, the value `consent_forms_bucket`, and `API_CONSENT_FORMS_PREFIX`, `consent-forms/`, the prefix under which `bootstrap.sh` grants `api-run` its right in that bucket: a constant of the module, not a variable, since the script writes it in its condition; a test holds the two equal ([`api` v0.1.27](https://github.com/altnovatrail/api/blob/v0.1.27/README.md));
  - `GOMEMLIMIT`, by the memory.
- **The jobs** — a `google_cloud_run_v2_job` for each entry of `api_jobs`: the command's arguments, secrets as files — each as a volume of its latest version, the path in `<NAME>_FILE`, as `api` reads any secret —, with no retries by the platform: whether to retry, the deployment decides. The field `deploy` is the job's place in the deployment: `check` — before the migrations, `migrate` — the migrations, `after` — when the new revision has taken all the traffic, and without the service it does not run; a job without it runs on a schedule, and the deployment only sets its image. The checks let through no other place, and none on a scheduled job. The default list:
  - `check`, `api check`, with no secrets of its own: by its place it gets what the revision will come out with — the facts volume, the first, at the same path, `API_ADMISSION_FACTS_FILE`, `API_ADMISSION_RUNG`, `API_READING_MODE`, `API_CATALOG_MODULES` and the variables of the store of the scans, whatever its field `consent_forms` says, and once —, and no key: `api check` calls no provider ([`api` v0.1.16](https://github.com/altnovatrail/api/blob/v0.1.16/README.md));
  - `migrate`, `api migrate`, with `api-migrate-database-url` and `api-migrate-openfga-database-url`, which the module creates without values;
  - `after`, `api after`, with the pooler's string of the database `api`, the service's secret, and a timeout of 900s, past its nine minutes; it reads the packages at `api`'s default `API_PACKAGES_DIR`, in the image. It runs after the traffic switch, so its failure leaves a new revision that already takes requests and a red run, and the next `sweep` closes what it did not ([`api` v0.1.20](https://github.com/altnovatrail/api/blob/v0.1.20/README.md));
  - `sweep`, `api sweep`, with both pooler's strings and the variables of the store of the scans ([`api` v0.1.22](https://github.com/altnovatrail/api/blob/v0.1.22/README.md)), a timeout of 900s, every day at 09:00 UTC, night in all the time zones of the USA, the platform's one region. Neither it nor the service sets `api`'s request deadlines, so both take `api`'s defaults, as `api` asks.

  A job that names a service's secret reads that secret, and the module makes no second; the facts secret in a job's `secrets` is refused, as are secrets by the names of the facts' volume and variables, which the module sets itself. A job added later is created with `api_image`, the first image, and gets the rolled-out one when the deployment sets the image of each job: a job enters the list with the release of `api` that has its command, and the deployment of that release comes after its `apply`. A secret the list no longer names the module lets go, `deletion_policy = "ABANDON"` ([`google_secret_manager_secret`](https://github.com/hashicorp/terraform-provider-google/blob/v8.10.0/website/docs/r/secret_manager_secret.html.markdown) at v8.10.0): it forgets it, and the value stays with whoever holds it.
- **The schedule** — the service account `api-scheduler` with `roles/run.invoker` on the scheduled jobs, and a `google_cloud_scheduler_job` that calls `…/jobs/<name>:run` in UTC; none of this exists while a job has no `schedule`, or before the service.
- **Service accounts and rights:**
  - `api-run` — the service and the jobs; it reads the facts secret, the key, the service's and the jobs' secrets;
  - `api-deploy` — the deployment: `roles/artifactregistry.writer` on `images`, `roles/secretmanager.secretVersionAdder` on the facts secret, `roles/iam.serviceAccountUser` on `api-run`, and the custom role `apiDeployer` — `run.services.get` and `.update`, `run.revisions.get` and `.list`, `run.operations.get`, `run.jobs.get`, `.update`, `.run` and `.runWithOverrides`, `run.executions.get` and `.list` — on the service and on each job, not on the project. `bootstrap.sh` creates and keeps the role, and the module grants it by the name `projects/<project>/roles/apiDeployer`; a permission in it changes by an edit of the script and a rerun before the merge, the script's test holds the set, and `tofu/tests/bootstrap.tftest.hcl` holds that the module grants the role the script creates. The deployment cannot create or delete services, change who invokes them, or read secrets itself; but what runs as `api-run` reads the secrets of `api`, and the service account that sets the image of `api` reaches them that way: the deployment is trusted with the same as `api`, and its door is one environment of one repository. On `novatrail` `api-run` has one role on the project, the module's `signInUsers`, and `api-deploy` none, and the deployment's rights on its own resources were enough;
  - the deployment is let in by `roles/iam.workloadIdentityUser` only for the subject `<github_oidc_subject_prefix>:environment:production` — the jobs of the environment `production` of the repository `infra`. The pool, its provider and the service account that applies the configuration are created by `bootstrap.sh`, and the module names them by ID;
  - `allUsers` invokes the service: whom to let in, the checks of `api` decide.
- **The size** — variables: from 0 to `api_max_instances`, 1 by default, 1 vCPU, `1Gi`, 40 requests per instance, and a request as long as `api`'s `API_HTTP_WRITE_TIMEOUT`, which the module does not set, so its default holds; it is over the 90 s of `FindTasks` and `SubmitWork`, a task from paper, the longest deadline of `api`'s methods ([`api` v0.1.72](https://github.com/altnovatrail/api/blob/v0.1.72/docs/build.md)). The values are `tofu/variables.tf`'s. The checks hold Cloud Run's limits: 1, 2, 4, 6 or 8 vCPU, no less than 2 GiB at 4 and 4 GiB at 6 and 8, a request up to 3600 s, a job up to 168 hours, and a job's timeout a whole number of seconds (sources 3.6).

— *Why:* everything but what each deployment changes lies in one place and is checked by tests; an image that is not in the registry is not made up; a job with an invented command is not created; the deployment's rights are exactly its steps, on its resources, and it runs the jobs by their place, not by name. The custom role is the script's because the configuration's service account cannot edit custom roles, or else the condition that limits what it may grant, `hostingPublisher` among it, holds nothing. `check` gets what the revision will come out with, so that facts or a store that `api` refuses stop the deployment before the migrations, not the first start of the revision after them. The schedule waits for the service: before it the migrations have not run, the service's secrets may have no value, and `sweep` would fail every day. The key is a volume at an exact version, so a revision pins the key it runs with. A request cut by the platform before `api`'s own deadline never gets `api`'s answer, and one held past it holds what `api` already let go.

— *Rejected:*
- Google's placeholder image `hello`, as at canvas-api: it is not a static Go binary on distroless, as an image in production must be, and would answer in production before the first deployment;
- the deployment creates the service and the jobs through `gcloud`, and the module keeps only the service accounts and the rights: the form of the service would leave OpenTofu for scripts;
- all the jobs at once, by names that `api` has not given yet, or by name in the module, or a map of variables in an entry of `api_jobs` — a list that holds only what exists, its data the module's, from which the deployment reads each job's place; a default cannot name the value of another variable, the bucket's;
- the deployment's rights on the project, or the predefined Cloud Run Developer role: it holds both the deletion of the service and changes of IAM;
- the service at once with the jobs — the first revision before the migrations — or the service at once but without the right of `allUsers` to invoke it — a revision before the migrations is running all the same and reads unchecked facts;
- handing the volumes and mounts to the deployment, `ignore_changes` on them whole: the module would no longer carry to the jobs the edits of their secrets;
- the key as a variable from the latest version, as the pooler's strings: the revision would not pin its key; the key for the jobs: `api check` calls no provider; both pooler's strings for `after`, which does not open `openfga`: an extra secret is extra access;
- `check` without the variables of the store of the scans: its line `admission` would say "none", and a store `api` refuses would stop the revision's first start after the migrations;
- a request shorter than `api`'s write timeout, such as 60 s: Cloud Run would cut `FindTasks` and `SubmitWork` before their 90 s, which `api` gave them over 60 s for a slow third attempt.

— *Consequence:* `tofu/api.tf`; `tofu/identities.tf`; `tofu/secrets.tf`; `tofu/variables.tf`; `tofu/outputs.tf`; `tofu/tests/api.tftest.hcl`; `tofu/tests/bootstrap.tftest.hcl`; `tofu/tests/copy.tftest.hcl`; `tofu/tests/terraform.tfvars`; `tools/deploy/api.sh`; `docs/architecture.md`; SELF-HOSTING.md.

## Firebase and publishing on Hosting

**R12. The module describes Firebase in the platform's project and its default Hosting site, `<project>.web.app`, and the module does not delete the site. The build of `app` is published by a service account of its own, `hosting-publish`, from a GitHub environment of its own, `production-hosting`: on the project it has only the custom role `hostingPublisher` — the predefined Firebase Hosting Admin without creating and deleting sites and without `resourcemanager.projects.list` —, which `bootstrap.sh` creates and keeps and the module grants. It reaches neither `api`, nor the configuration, nor the sign-in settings.** The model is `firebase.tf` and the service account `hosting` of canvas-api, commit 0901af0.
- **Firebase and the site** — `google_firebase_project` and `google_firebase_hosting_site` from `google-beta`: `google`, at the version `tofu/versions.tf` pins, does not have them (measurements 4.1). The site's ID is the project ID, and the address of `app` is `<project>.web.app`. For a project given Firebase after 15 October 2026 the default site is created on demand, and "The first Hosting site created in your Firebase project always becomes your default Hosting site"; for an older project the resource takes its site over (sources 4.6). `deletion_policy = "PREVENT"`: the module does not delete the address of `app`.
- **Publishing:**
  - `roles/iam.workloadIdentityUser` — only for the subject `<github_oidc_subject_prefix>:environment:production-hosting`; a check does not let through the same environment as that of the deployment of `api`;
  - on the project — the role `hostingPublisher` alone, by the name `projects/<project>/roles/hostingPublisher`: Hosting has no rights at the level of a site. It holds the permissions of Firebase Hosting Admin by the IAM reference without `firebasehosting.sites.create` and `.delete` (sources 4.6), seven: `firebase.clients.get` and `.list`, `firebase.projects.get`, `firebasehosting.sites.get`, `.list` and `.update`, and `resourcemanager.projects.get`. A role of a project cannot hold `resourcemanager.projects.list`: on the first run of `bootstrap.sh` on `novatrail` IAM refused to create the role with it — "INVALID_ARGUMENT: Permission resourcemanager.projects.list is not valid." —, and a release lists no projects. A release goes through `sites.update`: `tools/deploy/app.mjs` goes to the Hosting REST API by the site's name, reads no API keys, and on the project went through with this role alone, though Firebase writes that custom roles do not work for Hosting (sources 4.5). The publisher can neither delete nor make anew the site that is the address of `app`;
  - the role is the script's, not the module's: the configuration's service account may grant on the project only the roles the script names in an IAM condition, and Google says not to put into such a condition "Custom roles that the limited IAM admin can modify" (sources 5.3): a role that the service account edited itself it would extend with a permission `setIamPolicy` and grant to itself. So the module creates no custom roles, and the configuration's service account has no `iam.roleAdmin`. A permission in the role changes by an edit of the script;
  - it has no right on the sign-in settings. `firebaseauth.configs.update` cannot be narrowed to the authorized domains: with it, a job that releases the build could add a foreign domain and sign in to the platform's project from its own page, turn on the log of sign-ins or swap the email. The module itself names the authorized domains, `<project>.web.app` among them, as a full list: `infra-config` already holds `firebaseauth.configs.update` through `roles/firebase.admin`, and self-hosting on Google Cloud takes the same module.
- **Sign-in is apart.** Sign-in in production — Identity Platform, sign-in through Google, the OAuth client and its secret, the sign-in rights of `api-run`, the authorized domains — is decided apart from publishing: `api` sets up sign-in only in its own dev project, and who did it in production had been decided nowhere. The module does not turn on the export of Hosting's logs, and each sink excludes them.

— *Why:* with the service account of the deployment of `api`, the publishing job would also get the right to set the image of `api` and run its jobs; a service account and an environment of its own cut this off. The rights are exactly what a release needs, and a service account that releases the build keeps nothing extra.

— *Rejected:*
- publishing as `api-deploy` in the environment `production` — the argument above;
- the role Firebase Admin: it holds the whole of the project's Firebase, and only Hosting is needed;
- the predefined Firebase Hosting Admin: it holds creating and deleting sites, and the publisher could delete the site;
- `hostingPublisher` in the module, with the risk recorded: Google says plainly not to put such a role into the condition (sources 5.3);
- the reading of API keys, `roles/serviceusage.apiKeysViewer`, as at canvas-api: only `firebase deploy` needs it ([`infra/bootstrap.sh`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/infra/bootstrap.sh) of canvas-api), and the release reads none;
- the editing of the sign-in settings by `hosting-publish` for the sake of the authorized domains — the argument above;
- the authorized domains set by a step of the deployment: it would need one more service account with `firebaseauth.configs.update`, which `infra-config` already holds;
- sign-in together with publishing at once: it would grow beyond one check, and the decisions on the OAuth client would have to be made on the go.

— *Consequence:* `tofu/firebase.tf`; `tofu/identities.tf`; `tofu/logging.tf`; `tofu/bootstrap.sh`; `tofu/tests/firebase.tftest.hcl`; `tofu/tests/bootstrap.test.mjs`; `tofu/tests/gcloud-stub.mjs`; `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`; `docs/architecture.md`.

## The operator's one-time step

**R13. `tofu/bootstrap.sh` does once, with the operator's rights, what the configuration cannot do itself: the project and its link to the billing account, the state bucket, the Workload Identity Federation pool with the provider `github`, the configuration's service account `infra-config` with its rights, the three custom roles that the module grants, the second pool `github-eval` for the eval jobs, and the operator's bucket and the bucket of the scans of consents with all their access. The provider `github` lets in only tokens of the repository `infra` — by the numeric IDs of the owner and of the repository —, and only jobs of the GitHub environment `production-config` take `infra-config`. The roles of `infra-config` are listed, not Owner; it can change who holds what on the project only for `hostingPublisher` and `signInUsers`, and cannot edit any role.** The model is [`infra/bootstrap.sh`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/infra/bootstrap.sh) of canvas-api.
- **Where the values come from.** The script reads `project_id`, `region`, `workload_identity_pool_id`, `github_oidc_subject_prefix`, `eval_github_subject_prefixes` and `budget_currency` from `tofu/production.auto.tfvars`, and `bucket` from `tofu/backend.hcl`: the operator writes both files, and each value lies in one place. An empty value, `REPLACE…` or a value not of the form the module takes is a refusal before the first call of `gcloud`. The billing account comes from `TF_VAR_billing_account`, as in `plan`, and the script prints it nowhere, not even in an error of `gcloud`: there it is replaced by the words "<billing account>", as at canvas-api. The organizers' and the operator's addresses come from the environment too, each in a variable of its own, and nothing the script prints holds them. The operator runs it by `just bootstrap` where `gcloud` is — the container has none —, before the first `apply`.
- **Billing.** The account's currency is matched against `budget_currency` before anything is created: a budget in another currency would fail only at the first `apply`, after the merge. A project without billing is linked to `TF_VAR_billing_account`; a project that already pays through another account the script does not move, and stops: the budget counts the spending of the account the project pays through. A project marked for deletion the script does not touch either: bringing it back is a person's step.
- **The APIs** — `serviceusage`, `cloudresourcemanager`, `iam`, `sts`, `iamcredentials` and `storage`, without which nothing below can be created; `observability` in advance, as its settings refuse for a while after it is enabled (canvas-api); `run`, `firebase`, `firebasehosting` and `identitytoolkit`, before the roles, for the sake of the permissions of the custom roles: "a permission might not be available for use in custom roles if you have not enabled the API for the service" (sources 5.3).
- **The custom roles** — `apiDeployer`, the eleven permissions of the deployment of `api`, granted on its service and each of its jobs; `hostingPublisher`, the seven permissions of publishing, granted on the project; `signInUsers`, the sign-in role of `api-run`, granted on the project — `firebaseauth.users.get`, `.update` and `.delete`: sign-in gives no rights on anything smaller than the project, and the predefined Firebase Authentication Admin would also give `api` the editing of the sign-in settings. The roles of the buckets are below. The script creates them, restores one deleted recently, and sets the permissions, the title and the description of an existing one anew: `gcloud iam roles update --permissions` replaces the list whole (sources 5.3). A role is created only when the read answered `NOT_FOUND`; another error of the read the script retries, and does not take for the role's absence.
- **The state bucket** — in the region, with uniform access, without public access and with versioning: a version is what rolls back a state file written badly.
- **The provider `github`** — the issuer `https://token.actions.githubusercontent.com`, `google.subject=assertion.sub` and the condition `assertion.repository_owner_id=='<owner ID>' && assertion.repository_id=='<repository ID>'`, the numbers from `github_oidc_subject_prefix`. For `infra` it is `repo:altnovatrail@344507087/infra@1425816333`, with `use_immutable_subject` `true` (measurements 5.1). A name, unlike a number, can be taken by someone else if its owner lets it go. An existing provider gets its condition set anew, and a pool and a provider deleted recently are restored. Which service account a job may take, each service account decides by its binding by environment.
- **The pool `github-eval`.** When `eval_github_subject_prefixes` names the eval repositories, the script makes a second pool, `github-eval`, with a provider `github`, by the same function, `federated_pool`: its condition admits only jobs of the GitHub environment `eval` of those repositories, all of one owner, by the numeric IDs, and no Dependabot's. It lends no service account: the module grants its principals the eval secrets alone. When the list is empty, the provider of a pool made before gets the condition `false`, which admits nobody, and an edit of the list means rerunning the script before the merge. `infra-config` has no rights on the pools.
- **The rights of `infra-config`:**
  - on the project — `serviceusage.serviceUsageAdmin`, `browser`, `artifactregistry.admin`, `run.admin`, `iam.serviceAccountAdmin`, `iam.serviceAccountUser`, `secretmanager.admin`, `cloudscheduler.admin`, `firebase.admin`, `monitoring.notificationChannelEditor`, `monitoring.alertPolicyEditor`, `essentialcontacts.admin`, `logging.configWriter` and `observability.editor` — the set of canvas-api without `iam.roleAdmin`, with the roles of secrets, of Scheduler and of Essential Contacts, which canvas-api did not have; for each role the script names the module's resource types it is needed for. The line of `roles/firebase.admin` holds `google_identity_platform_config`, `google_firebase_web_app` and `data.google_firebase_web_app_config`: the role already has `firebase.clients.*` and `apikeys.keys.getKeyString`, which the Web App of `app` and its configuration need. `essentialcontacts.admin`, for `google_essential_contacts_contact`, the project's contact for Google's security notifications, is the one predefined role of Essential Contacts that writes a contact, `essentialcontacts.*` (sources 5.7);
  - `storage.objectAdmin` on the state bucket; `billing.costsManager` on the billing account, where the budget lives;
  - `resourcemanager.projectIamAdmin` under the condition `api.getAttribute('iam.googleapis.com/modifiedGrantsByRole', []).hasOnly(['projects/<project>/roles/hostingPublisher', 'projects/<project>/roles/signInUsers'])`: exactly the roles the module grants on the project, all by one list, `google_project_iam_member.grants`, and `just tf-check` lets no grant of rights on the project through outside it. The condition follows the IAM page Set limits on granting roles; it has neither a role with the permission `setIamPolicy` nor a custom role that `infra-config` can edit, as the page requires (sources 5.3); IAM accepted it on the project with roles of the form `projects/<project>/roles/…`. The role is held only under this expression: under another one or without a condition it is first taken away, as at canvas-api. Its line also names `google_project_iam_audit_config`: the apply writes the project's audit config of Secret Manager's reads under the condition, since an audit config modifies no role binding, which `modifiedGrantsByRole` counts (sources 2.6), and on the project it did — "google_project_iam_audit_config.secret_reads: Creation complete after 8s" —, so the role needs no widening. The project's `auditConfigs` were `null` before, so none of `allServices` or of Secret Manager joins or is replaced by the module's;
  - what `infra-config` holds on the project, on the bucket and on the account beyond what is named — under any condition too —, the script first takes away, and grants what is named after: `gcloud` removes a binding with all its conditions, since a condition to remove is named by its title too. The lists in the script are all it holds; on the project it holds exactly them, and the first `apply` passed without a refusal of rights;
  - `iam.serviceAccountAdmin` and `iam.serviceAccountUser` on the whole project: it creates the module's service accounts itself, and the right to decide who acts as a service account is the right to act as it. Through a service account of the project with Owner or Editor this would reach the editing of custom roles and the owner, and Google itself grants such a role to the default service accounts of Compute Engine and App Engine when an organization does not forbid it — the project has no organization (Д3); on the project an enabled Google API created the default Compute service account with Editor. So at each run the script takes Owner and Editor away from all the service accounts of the project itself — `*@<project>.iam.gserviceaccount.com`, `<number>-compute@developer.gserviceaccount.com` and `<project>@appspot.gserviceaccount.com` —, and does not touch Google's service agents or people; the module turns on neither Compute Engine nor App Engine. A window stays: a service account that got such a role between two runs holds it until the next;
  - Firebase's own service account `firebase-adminsdk-fbsvc`, which Firebase created at the first `apply` with `roles/firebase.sdkAdminServiceAgent` and `roles/iam.serviceAccountTokenCreator` on the project, stays: it is Firebase's default setup, it has no user-managed keys (`gcloud iam service-accounts keys list --managed-by=user` is empty), only Owner can take it, and `infra-config` manages the IAM of the project's service accounts anyway.
- **Who takes `infra-config`** — `roles/iam.workloadIdentityUser` only for `<github_oidc_subject_prefix>:environment:production-config`; any other binding on the service account itself — another principal that may impersonate it, `serviceAccountTokenCreator`, a binding under a condition — the script takes away. The environment `production-config` is its own, only `main` runs in it, and `apply` runs as `infra-config`: the script refuses if `deploy_environment` or `hosting_environment` in `production.auto.tfvars` matches it regardless of letter case, and the checks of these variables in the module do not let it through.
- **The buckets.** The script also makes and keeps the operator's bucket and the bucket of the scans of consents — their settings, their lifecycle rules, their custom roles, the two upload service accounts and the whole IAM policy of each bucket, the binding of `api-run` among it. It gives `infra-config` no role of Cloud Storage for them, and the condition `hasOnly` does not change: a bucket's policy is not the project's. But `roles/firebase.admin` of `infra-config` holds `storage.buckets.*` and `storage.objects.*` on the project (sources 10.6), so CI reads and changes the buckets all the same. This is accepted while only adults' files lie there; before the first child both buckets move to a project of the operator's own, where `infra-config` holds nothing (Д23), and until then the script and its condition do not change.
- **What it prints** — `GCP_WORKLOAD_IDENTITY_PROVIDER` and `GCP_CONFIG_SERVICE_ACCOUNT`, neither of them a secret, which go into the committed `tofu/ci.env`, where the deploy workflow reads them, as at canvas-api ([`infra/ci.env`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/infra/ci.env) of canvas-api); and what else to do in the repository's settings: the environment `production-config` and its secrets `TF_VAR_billing_account` and `TF_VAR_operator_email`.
- **A rerun** is safe: each step looks before it creates, and the provider's condition, the permissions of the custom roles, the roles of `infra-config`, the principals that may impersonate it and the buckets' settings, rules and policies are brought to what is written in the script. A new role that the module grants on the project first goes into the script's condition, and a new resource type into a role's line. The script is run again when it changes — before the merge that needs it.
- **The policy version.** Which version of a policy `gcloud` reads it chooses itself: "These tools specify the version automatically" (sources 5.3). In version 1 IAM "appends the string `_withcond_` to the role name, followed by a hash value" and gives no conditions, so the IAM admin role is matched by the start of its name, as at canvas-api. On the project `gcloud` returns the policy in version 3, and the binding of `projectIamAdmin` comes with its condition, not with `_withcond_`.
- **The tests:**
  - `tofu/tests/bootstrap.test.mjs` runs the script on a stub `gcloud`, `tofu/tests/gcloud-stub.mjs`, with synthetic values: what is created, the roles and the conditions, the custom roles with their exact permissions, that a rerun creates nothing and brings back what drifted, that refusals come before the first call of `gcloud`, and that the billing account gets into the output in no error;
  - `tofu/tests/bootstrap.tftest.hcl` matches the roles the module grants on the project against the roles of the script's condition, and the names of the custom roles in the module against the IDs in the script, as canvas-api's test does, and the module lets the configuration's environment from the script go neither to the deployment of `api` nor to publishing;
  - `_tf-roles` in `tf-check`: each resource and data type in `tofu/*.tf` is named under some role of the script, and each named type is still in the module;
  - ShellCheck passes the script with no findings, in CI's `ci-shellcheck`.

— *Why:*
- the pool, the provider and the configuration's service account are the door through which the configuration gets in, and it is not built by whoever gets in through it; the state bucket is needed before the first `init`;
- a list of roles instead of Owner keeps `infra-config` in one project and without the right to hand out roles;
- an environment of its own cuts it off from the jobs of the deployment of `api` and of publishing: the first runs `docker` and `gcloud`, the second `tools/deploy/app.mjs`;
- the custom roles are in the script, not in the module: `infra-config` grants `hostingPublisher` and `signInUsers` under a condition that holds only if it cannot edit the role it grants (sources 5.3);
- the two printed lines are no secret, and in a file review sees them.

— *Rejected:*
- a plan on a pull request by the same service account, as canvas-api's `pull_request`: a pull request's job can rewrite itself and get the configuration's rights before review. If a plan on a pull request is needed, it takes a read-only service account of its own;
- the environment `production`, shared with `api-deploy`: any job of the deployment of `api` could take the configuration too;
- pinning one workflow in the provider's condition, `assertion.job_workflow_ref`, as canvas-api did: the bindings by environment already let in only jobs of environments where only `main` runs, and the eval jobs of other repositories get in through a pool of their own;
- the pool and the provider in the module: the configuration gets in through them, and an `apply` that broke them could not fix them;
- the pool `github-eval` in the module, or any right of `infra-config` on the pools: IAM conditions do not narrow pools (sources 5.6), and a right on one would be a right on the one through which it gets in;
- the custom roles in the module, and `iam.roleAdmin` for `infra-config`: it could then add any permission to `hostingPublisher` or `signInUsers`, which it grants, and so become the project's owner;
- the predefined Firebase Authentication Admin for `api-run`: it would also give `api` the editing of the sign-in settings, which the configuration holds;
- Owner or Editor: the first has the project's whole policy, the second almost everything in all services;
- the two printed lines as GitHub variables: the values would live in the settings, past review.

— *Consequence:* `tofu/bootstrap.sh`; `tofu/identities.tf`; `tofu/ci.env`; `tofu/tests/bootstrap.test.mjs`; `tofu/tests/gcloud-stub.mjs`; `tofu/tests/bootstrap.tftest.hcl`; `justfile`; `.github/workflows/ci.yml`; `docs/architecture.md`.

## The checks of the rollout list

**R14. The rollout list is checked by the schema `deploy/schema/production.schema.json` and by the check `tools/deploy-check/`, in Node: the list by its schema; the name of `api`'s binary, which the schema cannot express; the release files, by `release-verify` and sha256; `deployment.json`, by `spec`'s reading of the deployment configuration and by the schema of its build, with the build's layout, its notices, its CSP and the address of `api`'s service; the facts, by the vocabulary of `spec`'s admission table; a copy's own legal texts, by `spec`'s `legal-check-dir`; the rung, the configuration's, and `full` for the facts of a learner under 18; the tag of `api` no lower than on `main`. In CI the job `Deploy`, in the stage `toolchain-core`, runs its tests in every pull request, and the list itself when a pull request changes what checks it. The dependencies are ajv, and `yaml` for the tests alone, by the lock file of `tools/`.**
- **The schema** — JSON Schema 2020-12, format 1; a field the schema does not know is an error at every level. It holds the form: exact tags `vX.Y.Z`, sha256 of 64 lowercase hexadecimal digits, the binary's name `api_<tag>_linux_amd64`, the name of `app`'s archive — `*.tar.gz` with no path, the rung — `author` or `full`, both rungs of `tutor`. What the schema cannot express, the check holds: the tag in the binary's name equals the release's tag.
- **The check** — `node tools/deploy-check/main.mjs [--base <ref>] [<directory>]`, the recipe `just deploy-check`; without `deploy/production.json` it says there is nothing to check. Each refusal names what was found and what was expected. Each check runs whatever the earlier ones found, and all the refusals are named, not the first: each release file by a check of its own, the sha256 of `deployment.json` by its own; a check that needs a rejected file says it did not run. The exception is the schema's refusal: after it the list cannot be read further. A pull request does not delete a list that exists on the base: otherwise the next list would have nothing to be compared with, and `api` would go back. The `just` by which the check runs `spec`'s `release-verify` is the one that ran the recipe: the `justfile` hands its absolute path in `INFRA_JUST`, and without it it is `/usr/local/bin/just` of the toolchain stages: on a runner of `Deploy`, `tools/ci/install.sh` puts `just` into a folder of its own. The path is still absolute, and `PATH` does not decide which program runs:
  - the release files are downloaded from the release's public address on GitHub together with `.sigstore.json`; the sha256 is compared with the one the list names, and `just -f spec/justfile release-verify` accepts the file. If GitHub or Sigstore do not answer, the file is rejected;
  - `deployment.json` — by the reading of the `spec` submodule, `tools/deploy-check/reading.mjs`: the schema `spec/deployment/schemas/deployment.schema.json`, then the rules `deployment.api-origin` and `deployment.loopback` (`spec/deployment/README.md`). The origin of `app` here is `https://web.app`: the pull request's check does not know the site, and the platform's `app` is never on a loopback host. This check runs even when the build is rejected. Then by the schema `deployment.schema.json` from the root of this build's archive, and a schema of a dialect other than 2020-12 is a refusal; the build's version, which the `$id` of its `deployment.schema.json` names, must be the version of the `spec` submodule, or it is a refusal. The file is no larger than 16 KiB;
  - `app`'s build — the archive is unpacked only if its listing showed nothing but files and folders, with no paths outward; at its root, as files and folders, `index.html`, `assets/` with a file, `deployment.schema.json`, `csp.json`, `NOTICE` and `LICENSE`, and no `deployment.json` of its own (`spec/docs/decisions.md`): a missing one is a refusal that names it, and an extra file is not. `csp.json` with this `deployment.json` gives the CSP header. These are the same functions of `check.mjs` as the release of the build's;
  - the address of `api` in `deploy/production/deployment.json` — the address of the service `api`, the output `api_origin`, `https://<api_service_name>-<project number>.<region>.run.app` (sources 3.9), built from the files the module builds it from: the project number from the path of the pool's provider in `GCP_WORKLOAD_IDENTITY_PROVIDER` of `tofu/ci.env`, `region` and `api_service_name` from `tofu/production.auto.tfvars`, and, where the name is not there, from the default in `tofu/variables.tf`. Another address is a refusal, with both named. Without `tofu/ci.env`, or outside a git repository, the check says it did not compare: there is no project. If the file is there but the number, the region or the name cannot be read from it, the check itself refuses. Both files are read as the deployment reads them: `tofu/ci.env` as lines `KEY=value`, a repeated key a refusal, as in `identity()`; a value by the line that starts with the variable's name, as `tofu fmt` writes it, while an indented line is a key inside another value; a default by the line `default` of the block, two spaces in. The provider's pattern and the parsing of both files are `tools/deploy-check/setup.mjs`, with no imports, which `identity()` and the deployment's `setsVariable` (`tools/deploy/target.mjs`) both take, so the steps of the workflow `Deploy` that call it install no packages. The comparison after the merge, by the release of `app`, stays as a second layer: it reads the module's outputs that are applied, not the commit's files, and catches a release run by hand. On `main` it finds `api` at `https://api-306463944576.us-east4.run.app`;
  - the facts — by `$defs/declared_facts` of the schema of the answer vector in `spec/admission/schemas/`, and the providers and the codes of the facts from the vocabulary of `spec/admission/admission-table.json`, at the submodule's tag. This is an early sign: `api` checks the file by its own table at its start;
  - a copy's `deploy/production/legal/`, if there is one — nothing but files and folders, and `just -f spec/justfile legal-check-dir production/legal` passes, run from the deploy folder; with no folder the item says so and passes;
  - the rung — the one the configuration tells `api`, `api_admission_rung` of `tofu/production.auto.tfvars` or its default in `tofu/variables.tf`, and `full` wherever the facts name a category under 18 (`spec/docs/decisions.md`);
  - the tag of `api` is compared with the tag of the list on the base by numbers, not as text: v0.10.0 is higher than v0.9.0. A base without a list has nothing to compare with.
- **ajv** — with the settings of `spec` itself (`spec/tools/format-check/schemas.mjs`): all errors; an unknown keyword is an error; `format` only an annotation, as in 2020-12 by default; `strictTypes` and `strictTuples` off — otherwise ajv rejects `spec`'s admission schemas. The second package, `yaml`, is in `devDependencies`: only the tests on `spec`'s reading vectors take it, and the code of the checks and of the deployment does not import it. The licenses: MIT for ajv, `fast-deep-equal`, `json-schema-traverse` and `require-from-string`, BSD-3-Clause for `fast-uri`, ISC for `yaml`; `just ci-licenses` — `licenses-check-repo` of `spec` — lets all six through without exceptions, the evidence of their licenses. Dependabot raises the packages: the ecosystem `npm`, the directory `/tools`.
- **Tests:**
  - `tools/deploy-check/test/check.test.mjs` — synthetic, without the network: the releases are files in a directory, `release-verify` and `legal-check-dir` are stand-ins, the test builds `app`'s archive itself; each refusal one at a time, a refusal that does not hide the others, a deleted list and a pass. The test gives its stubs to `run()` as parameters: the command line, `main.mjs`, does not take them, and no environment variable will replace the real `release-verify`. The refusals by address — another number, another region, the default name where the values name another — and the key `region` inside another value each have a test that fails without its rule;
  - `tools/deploy-check/test/reading.test.mjs` — the reading of `deployment.json` on `spec`'s reading vectors;
  - `tools/deploy-check/test/release.test.mjs` — a real release, `api` v0.1.5: the binary and the `Dockerfile` from GitHub pass `release-verify` against Sigstore and the comparison with the sha256 from the release's `SHA256SUMS`, a copy of the `Dockerfile` with one byte changed, under the same signature, is rejected, and a file that is not in the release the check does not find.
- **CI** — the job `Deploy`, in the stage `toolchain-core`, since its recipes need none of the three tools that `toolchain` adds: `ci-licenses`, `deploy-check-test`, `deploy-check-release-test` and `ci-deploy-check`, which calls `deploy-check` against `origin/main` only if a pull request changes what the list is checked by: `deploy/`, `tools/deploy-check/`, `tools/deploy/`, the packages of `tools/`, the `justfile`, the tag of the `spec` submodule, whose admission table and `release-verify` the check reads, `tofu/variables.tf`, whose default names the service, or the line of `tofu/production.auto.tfvars` that names the rung. The checkout is with the whole history, so that there is a list on `main` to compare with.

— *Why:*
- `release-verify` and the other checks of `spec` are in Node, and ajv of one version checks `spec`'s schemas, the build's schema and the list's schema alike;
- the test on a real release catches what the synthetic one does not see: the release's address, the bundle's name and `release-verify` itself; its price — every pull request waits for GitHub and Sigstore and turns red when they do not answer — is accepted;
- checking the list itself only when what checks it is edited keeps a pull request of documents from downloading the release files — `api`'s binary and `app`'s archive;
- the rollout is a reader of `deployment.json` too, and `spec`'s vectors keep its reading beside the reading of `app`;
- a build that the release would reject is better rejected before the merge; without the check of `NOTICE` and `LICENSE` an archive without the license notices would pass the rollout, and the site would serve the code of React, Firebase and others without the notices their licenses require;
- the address of `api` checked before the merge — otherwise a foreign address passes the review, only the release of `app` after the merge refuses it, and `app` waits for a second pull request.

— *Rejected:*
- Go — `release-verify` is in Node anyway, and the checks would end up in two languages;
- ajv from `spec/tools/node_modules` — the checks would depend on how `spec`'s tools are built inside, and a new tag could break them;
- checking the list in every pull request — even an edit of documents would download the release files and check each one;
- the test on a real release only by hand — a breakage of the check on real signatures would be seen only by whoever ran it;
- looking `just` up by `PATH` — the check would run whichever program lies first on the path; putting `just` on the runner into `/usr/local/bin` — the installation of the tools would write into the runner's system directory for the sake of one path;
- adding `tofu/ci.env` and `tofu/production.auto.tfvars` as a whole to the paths of `ci-deploy-check` — a fork's first pull request changes them, without `variables.tf`, while its list is still the platform's, with the platform's address: the check would refuse, and the fork's first `apply` would not go through. An edit of the region or of the service's name without an edit of the list is caught by the release of `app`;
- the project number from `tofu output` — CI of a pull request has no access to production;
- the service's name without the default — a repeat of `variables.tf`, and an edit of the default would slip past the check.

— *Consequence:* `deploy/schema/production.schema.json`; `tools/deploy-check/`; `tools/deploy/target.mjs`; `tools/package.json`; `tools/package-lock.json`; `justfile`; `.github/workflows/ci.yml`; `.github/dependabot.yml`; `.claude/rules/deploy.md`; `docs/architecture.md`; SELF-HOSTING.md.

## The deployment of `api`

**R15. The deployment of `api` by the list is done by `tools/deploy/api.sh` — bash, by the steps of the rollout of a new release, on what the module's outputs name. The commands: `image` — the release's image and its digest; `release` — a new release in full; `facts` — only the facts. The tag of the release that takes the traffic is the tag of its image in the registry, and a copy's own legal texts are laid over the release's image under a tag of their own. Which jobs to run and when is said by the job's part in `api_jobs`, not by its name, and a release does not go without a job of the part `check` and one of `migrate`. The jobs `check` get the image and the exact version of the facts the revision will mount before they run.**
- **Values** — from `tofu output -json` of the same commit after `apply`: `project_id`, `region`, `image_repository`, `api_service_name`, `api_service_url`, `api_jobs`, `admission_facts_secret`, `admission_facts_file`. A missing output, and jobs of the wrong form, are a refusal before the first step. Whether the service exists is said by the outputs — `api_service_url`, as the release of `app` reads it —, not by an error of `gcloud`: the right of `api-deploy` to read the service is on the service itself, and about a service that does not exist Cloud Run answers it `PERMISSION_DENIED` "(or resource may not exist)", not `NOT_FOUND`. A service that the outputs name and `gcloud` does not read is a refusal: taken for "no service", it would skip the revision silently.
- **`image`:**
  - the files of `api`'s release are downloaded and checked again — `tools/deploy-check/fetch.mjs`, the same `releaseFile` as the list check's: sha256 and `release-verify`;
  - the binary — as the file `api` in the build context `binary`, the main context empty: `docker build --target release --build-context binary=<folder> -f <release Dockerfile>` ([`api` v0.1.5](https://github.com/altnovatrail/api/blob/v0.1.5/README.md));
  - the image — for `linux/amd64`, the platform of the binary and of Cloud Run, into `<registry>/api:<release tag>`, and the digest is the one the push reported. The tag keeps the image from the registry's cleanup and says which release it is;
  - an image that the registry already holds under the release's tag — from an earlier deployment of the same release — is taken as it is: built anew, it would get another digest, and the tag would leave an image that may be taking the traffic. A registry without the package `api` is a registry without tags: before the first push `gcloud artifacts docker tags list` answers `NOT_FOUND`, and `image` reads it just so; any other error is a refusal;
  - a copy's `deploy/production/legal/`, when there is one, is laid over the release's image, under the tag `<release>-legal-<sha256 of the folder>`, and `image`, `release` and `facts` give the revision and the jobs that image; the release that takes the traffic is told by the tag before `-legal-`. A folder with anything but files and folders in it is refused before anything runs.
- **`release`** — the steps in order, and the failure of any of them stops the deployment:
  1. if the service exists, the tag of the release that takes the traffic is the tag in the registry of the digest on which the revision with the traffic stands. No tag, more than one, a tag that is not exact, or traffic split between revisions — a refusal with its reason. A lower new release is a refusal; the same one is allowed. The tags are compared by numbers. The deployment refuses before the first step a list of jobs without the part `migrate` or without one of the part `check`;
  2. `image`;
  3. a new version of the facts secret, from `deploy/production/facts.json`;
  4. the image — to the jobs `check` and `migrate`, and only to them: a scheduled job or `after` with the new image would run the new release on a database that the migrations may not have reached. To the jobs `check` the image and the version of the facts just added go by one `gcloud run jobs update --image=… --update-secrets=<facts file>=<secret>:<version>`, as to the revision: overrides at execution do not change volumes, since `gcloud run jobs execute` overrides only `--args`, `--update-env-vars`, `--tasks` and `--task-timeout` (sources 3.5), and the right is `run.jobs.update` of the deployment's own role (sources 3.6);
  5. the jobs `check`, then `migrate` — each to its end, `gcloud run jobs execute --wait`;
  6. a revision by digest with this version of the facts, `--update-secrets <facts file>=<secret>:<version>`, without traffic; when the revision is ready — its own condition `Ready`, the revision being the service's `latestCreatedRevisionName` after `update` —, all the traffic to it, by name, `update-traffic --to-revisions=<revision>=100`: a revision created meanwhile by someone else will get no traffic. The service's `latestReadyRevisionName` does not tell it: with `--no-traffic` Cloud Run leaves it on the revision that serves;
  7. the image — to the jobs `after` and the scheduled ones: a job added with the new release was created by the module with the first image; then the jobs `after`. A failure of `after` is the same as a failure of any job of the deployment: `run_jobs` says "the job api-after did not end well, and the deployment stops here", and the job `api` of the workflow is red. Only, the deployment already stands on the new revision, which has taken all the traffic, and the release of `app` in that run does not go.

  While the module has not created the service — `api_image` set, `api_serving` not yet — steps 1, 6 and the jobs `after` are skipped, all the jobs get the image, and the recipe says so.
- **`facts`** — a new version of the secret, the jobs `check` with it on the image of the release that takes the traffic, and only then a new revision on that image, by its digest: without a build, migrations or `release-verify`. It reads the tag of the image that takes the traffic: with no texts in it, it lays a copy's folder, if any, over that digest; with texts, over the image of the tag of its release; and it gives the other jobs the new image when it changed. Without the service, or without a job of the part `check`, a refusal.
- **Recipes** — `just deploy-api-image`, `deploy-api`, `deploy-api-facts` with the path of the outputs; they run without GitHub Actions too, for self-hosting. `gcloud` and `docker` come from the environment where the deployment runs them.
- **Test** — `tools/deploy/test/api.test.mjs`: the script runs from a copy of the repository's layout in which `fetch.mjs` is replaced, and `gcloud` and `docker` are stubs, `tools/deploy/test/stub.mjs`. The script itself has no stub of `release-verify`. The test checks the order of the steps, the image of each job, the version of the facts on the jobs `check`, the refusals, the copy's folder and the path `facts`. ShellCheck checks `tools/deploy/*.sh` too.
- **The flags of `gcloud`** are those of its reference (sources 3.5). What the reference does not say, the project showed: `secrets versions add` with `--format='value(name)'` prints the version's full name, `…/versions/<number>`; `jobs execute --wait` with a job that failed exits non-zero, and the deployment stops before the revision — on a failed `api-migrate`, "ERROR: (gcloud.run.jobs.execute) The execution failed.", then "deploy-api: the job api-migrate did not end well, and the deployment stops here"; `--update-secrets` by path on an unnamed container makes a volume of its own, and on a named one edits the module's volume in place, on the revision and on the job `check` alike. A failure of `after` cannot be caused in a run of `Deploy` without an edit of the module — the job `config` before the deployment gives the job back its arguments `api after` —, and it stands by the code and the failure of `api-migrate`.

— *Why:*
- the deployment sets the image's tag anyway, and in the registry it stands beside the digest, while a label or a variable of the revision the module would erase at the next `apply`: it holds the service's variables as a set, and `ignore_changes` does not address one of them (measurements 4.1);
- the job's part is the module's data, and a command that `api` gives later joins the list together with its part, without an edit of the recipe;
- the migrations do not run without `api`'s check of the facts and the rung: without the refusal a list of jobs with only `migrate` would roll out a release having checked nothing;
- the exact version on the jobs `check` — the version checked is the one that goes into the revision;
- the existence of the service by the outputs — Cloud Run tells `api-deploy` no `NOT_FOUND` about a service it may not read;
- the deployment's steps are a chain of calls of `gcloud` and `docker`, and bash keeps it shorter, as in `bootstrap.sh`.

— *Rejected:*
- a label of the revision with the release — the module would erase it while `ignore_changes` does not hold it;
- the revision's variable `API_RELEASE` — the module holds the service's variables as a set;
- the commands' names in the recipe, or only `migrate` — code is not written by a name `api` has not given, and the recipe would have to be rewritten with each new command;
- recipes in Node;
- the values from `production.auto.tfvars`, as `bootstrap.sh` takes them — the recipe would repeat the module's rules: the jobs' names and the variables' defaults;
- `latest` at execution, with deployments one at a time (`concurrency: deploy`) — a version added by hand between `add` and the execution would be checked instead of the one that goes into the revision.

— *Consequence:* `tools/deploy/api.sh`; `tools/deploy/test/api.test.mjs`; `tools/deploy/test/stub.mjs`; `tools/deploy-check/fetch.mjs`; `tofu/variables.tf`; `tofu/outputs.tf`; `justfile`; `.github/workflows/ci.yml`; `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`; `docs/architecture.md`.

## Sign-in in production

**R16. Sign-in in the platform's production project is Firebase Auth with Identity Platform. The module moves the project onto it and holds its settings: the authorized domains — `<project>.firebaseapp.com` and `<project>.web.app`, sign-in only by Google's window, the activity log off. The operator turns on sign-in through Google, with its OAuth client and consent screen, in the Firebase console after the first `apply`: the client's secret is neither in the repository nor in the state. `api-run` manages the sign-in's users by its custom role `signInUsers` from `bootstrap.sh`, and learns the project from `API_FIREBASE_PROJECT_ID`.** How it is built is in [architecture.md](architecture.md). There is no model: canvas-api at commit 0901af0 has no sign-in in code, only decisions about it.
- **Identity Platform** — `google_identity_platform_config` in `tofu/sign_in.tf`, from the provider `google`, and `identitytoolkit.googleapis.com` in the list of APIs:
  - the settings of Firebase Auth are written in code only through it: the resource "enable[s] Google Cloud Identity Platform (GCIP) (which is the backend for Firebase Authentication)", and only in a project on Blaze (sources 4.7). On the project its creation, `initializeAuth`, let `infra-config` through;
  - the move cannot be undone, and in Google Cloud the resource is not deleted: `destroy` only removes it from the state, and the provider does not read `deletion_policy` ([`resource_identity_platform_config.go`](https://github.com/hashicorp/terraform-provider-google/blob/v8.10.0/google/services/identityplatform/resource_identity_platform_config.go) at v8.10.0);
  - the price: sign-in through Google is Tier 1, free up to 50,000 active users a month, then $0.0055 for each. After the move a project on Blaze pays "for usage beyond the free tier of 50,000 monthly active users" (sources 4.7).
- **Settings:**
  - the authorized domains are the whole list: the Firebase address on which `app`'s sign-in window opens (`authDomain`, `app`'s [`docs/decisions.md`](https://github.com/altnovatrail/app/blob/375a09b/docs/decisions.md) at 375a09b), and the origin of `app`. A domain added in the console the next `apply` will remove, and the domain of a Hosting preview channel too: `app` is released only to the site's live channel, the release of the build does not touch the domains, and a domain of its own will join the list once it is bought. The module takes the `web.app` address from the ID of the Hosting site, not from the project's ID, so that the two addresses do not diverge. Neither `localhost` nor `127.0.0.1`: with them the sign-in window of the platform's project would open from any local page, and the local run has a Firebase project of its own. On the project the sign-in window on `<project>.web.app` opens under the author's Google account and under another's, each making an account by an invite code;
  - sign-in by email, by phone and anonymous sign-in are off: the sign-in service holds no password, no email and no phone number, as `api` decided it. Duplicate email addresses are off, and so is the activity log, explicitly and not by default, and every sink excludes it. There are no tenants and no second factor;
  - `disabled_user_deletion = true`: only `api` deletes a user, together with the user's account and after revoking the tokens, as `api` decided it, and `app` deletes an account through `api`. Sign-up through Google's window is open, `disabled_user_signup = false`.
- **Sign-in through Google** — the operator's step, in the Firebase console after the first `apply`:
  - the OAuth client and the consent screen cannot be created programmatically: Google has shut the API that created them — "The IAP OAuth Admin API was shut down on March 19, 2026" — and the provider removed `google_iap_brand` and `google_iap_client` in 8.0.0 ([`version_8_upgrade`](https://github.com/hashicorp/terraform-provider-google/blob/v8.10.0/website/docs/guides/version_8_upgrade.html.markdown) at v8.10.0). `google_iam_oauth_client` is only for Workforce Identity Federation (sources 4.7);
  - the provider Google in the module, `google_identity_platform_default_supported_idp_config`, would hold the client's secret in the state in plain text: `client_secret` is not marked sensitive and is read back from the API, and there is no write-only variant ([`resource_identity_platform_default_supported_idp_config.go`](https://github.com/hashicorp/terraform-provider-google/blob/v8.10.0/google/services/identityplatform/resource_identity_platform_default_supported_idp_config.go) at v8.10.0);
  - on turning Google on, the Firebase console creates the OAuth client itself — its ID starts with the project number — and asks for no form with the client's ID and secret. After the step there are still two authorized domains, and `tofu plan` gives "No changes": the step changes the sign-in provider, not what the module holds;
  - the default scopes are enough for the consent screen — email, profile, openid — and they need no app verification (sources 4.7). A user will see the app's name and logo only after brand verification, and before it the address; brand verification comes with a domain of its own, after О-16. The Audience of Google Auth Platform is External and In production: the API does not read this setting, and the console shows it. The limit "0 users / 100 user cap" on the same page concerns only unverified sensitive scopes, and the sign-in has none;
  - no sign-in left an entry in Admin Activity.
- **`api-run`** — the role `signInUsers` on the project, with three rights, for the calls of `api`'s port of sign-in (sources 4.7; [`api` v0.1.6](https://github.com/altnovatrail/api/blob/v0.1.6/docs/decisions.md)):
  - `firebaseauth.users.get` — for the revocation check and the list of users, `accounts:lookup` and `accounts:batchGet`;
  - `firebaseauth.users.update` — for revoking tokens, `accounts:update`;
  - `firebaseauth.users.delete` — for deletion, `accounts:delete`.

  The token's signature is checked against Google's public keys, and that needs no rights. All three rights may be put into a custom role — "SUPPORTED" (sources 4.7). `bootstrap.sh` creates the role, and the condition of the configuration's service account names it.
- **`API_FIREBASE_PROJECT_ID`** — `var.project_id`, on the service and on every job: the Firebase project is the platform's project, and the operator's job of `api` goes through the sign-in's users.

— *Why:*
- production is on Blaze, and `api`'s argument against the move — the Spark plan's limit of 3,000 users a day (sources 4.7; [`api` v0.1.6](https://github.com/altnovatrail/api/blob/v0.1.6/docs/decisions.md)) — does not apply here, and the extra features of Identity Platform are off;
- the settings in code keep off what `api` rejected for sign-in — email, phone, the activity log — rather than relying on the default;
- the domains in the module bring no new holder of `configs.update`: the configuration's service account has that right anyway;
- the client's secret is neither in the repository nor in the state.

— *Rejected:*
- Firebase Auth without the move, as `api` decided it for itself — the settings would stay in the console, and the module would hold neither the domains nor the email turned off;
- the provider Google in the module — the client's secret in the state;
- the predefined `roles/firebaseauth.admin` for `api-run` — with it `api` would edit the sign-in settings;
- the domains as a step of the deployment — one more service account with `configs.update`, a right that cannot be narrowed to the domains;
- `localhost` or `127.0.0.1` in the domains of production — the argument above.

— *Not checked:* whether the three rights are enough for `api` without `serviceusage.services.use`.

— *Consequence:* `tofu/sign_in.tf`; `tofu/identities.tf`; `tofu/api.tf`; `tofu/apis.tf`; `tofu/bootstrap.sh`; `tofu/tests/sign_in.tftest.hcl`; `tofu/tests/bootstrap.tftest.hcl`; `tofu/tests/bootstrap.test.mjs`; `docs/architecture.md`.

## The release of the build of `app`

**R17. The build of `app` is released onto Firebase Hosting by a script of its own, `tools/deploy/app.mjs`, through the REST API of Hosting, not the Firebase CLI. It recognizes the released build by the release's message and keeps its `assets/` in the new version. Before the first call of Hosting it refuses a build the pull request check refuses, and a release before the service `api` or with another `api`. After the release it checks the site's answers in rounds, for up to 120 s. It puts the CSP header together from the build's `csp.json` — a table "directive → string of sources" with the places `{api}` and `{authDomain}` — and rejects any other form. The module creates the Web App, which gives `apiKey` and `appId` for `/deployment.json`; the key is no secret, and the shared secrets check lets it through in the list's `deployment.json` alone.** How it is built is in [architecture.md](architecture.md).
- **The steps of `release`:**
  1. the list — by its schema, and `production/deployment.json` — of the sha256 that the list has, and no more than the 16 KiB that `app` reads (its [`docs/architecture.md`](https://github.com/altnovatrail/app/blob/375a09b/docs/architecture.md) at 375a09b). These are the same functions of `check.mjs` as the pull request check's;
  2. the new build's archive — again `releaseFile` from `check.mjs`, sha256 and `release-verify`. Unpacking comes only after the archive's listing showed nothing but files and folders, with no links and no paths outward; at the root — `index.html`, `assets/` with a file, `deployment.schema.json`, `csp.json`, `NOTICE` and `LICENSE`, and no `deployment.json` of its own. `deployment.json` — by the build's schema and by the reading of `spec`'s submodule, with the build's version, in the same order as the pull request check; the origin of `app` is taken from the module's output `hosting_url`, and it must be `https`. Then the module's outputs: a refusal while they have no service `api`, `api_service_url` null, and when `api` in `deployment.json` is not the output `api_origin`. All of this comes before the first call of Hosting;
  3. the previous build — by the message of the live channel's release, `app <tag> <archive> <sha256>` and, after `keeping`, the build whose `assets/` it kept. Its archive passes the same checks, and its `assets/` go into the new version; a file of the same path is taken from the new build. The upload goes eight files at a time;
  4. `deployment.json` from the list;
  5. the version — `versions.create` with the configuration, `populateFiles` in batches of 1000 paths, the upload of what Hosting asked for, `FINALIZED`, and `releases.create` with this build's message. Hosting names a file by the sha256 of its bytes in gzip, and a version is a full set of files (sources 4.5);
  6. the check of the answers.
- **`NOTICE` and `LICENSE`** — at the root of the build's archive, as `spec` decided it, and "the site serves them at `/NOTICE` and `/LICENSE`"; the kept build passes the same check, and `app` had no release before them, so no earlier build lacks them.
- **The version's configuration** — two sets of paths that do not overlap, so that no header depends on which of two rules Hosting applies:
  - outside `/assets/**`, `!/@(assets)/**` — `nosniff`, HSTS, CSP and `Cache-Control: no-cache`;
  - under `/assets/**` — `nosniff`, HSTS and `public, max-age=31536000, immutable`;
  - the rewrite to `/index.html` — also outside `/assets/**`. Hosting rewrites only a path with no file, and `/deployment.json` is in every version, so it needs no exception of its own;
  - no `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy`. Hosting "overwrites the Strict-Transport-Security configuration on default Hosting subdomains" (sources 4.5); a header of its own is for a domain of its own.

  It holds on production as written, with no explicit list of SPA paths, by `curl -D -` to `<project>.web.app` and `<project>.firebaseapp.com`, which serve the same bytes and headers:
  - production accepts `!/@(assets)/**`: `/`, an SPA path, `/deployment.json` and `/NOTICE` have CSP, `nosniff` and `no-cache`, and a file of `/assets/` `immutable` and `nosniff`, without CSP. An unknown file under `/assets/` is a 404 with Hosting's own "Page Not Found" page and the headers of the paths outside `/assets/**`, not the rewrite;
  - Hosting's own HSTS on both addresses is `max-age=31556926; includeSubDomains; preload`. Beyond the version it sends `etag`, `last-modified`, `accept-ranges`, `vary`, `alt-svc` and the cache headers `x-served-by`, `x-cache`, `x-cache-hits` and `x-timer`, and no `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy`;
  - the reserved paths `/__/auth/*`, Firebase's sign-in pages and not files of the build, Hosting serves itself with only HSTS and `cache-control: max-age=1800`, without CSP and `nosniff`; the version does not set them.
- **CSP** — each directive of the table, with the places filled in from `deployment.json`: `{api}` — the origin of `api`, `{authDomain}` — the host of the sign-in window. `frame-ancestors` is `'none'`, and a table without it gets it. A refusal — not a table, a name that is not a directive, a value not as one string or with `;` and `,`, another place, `frame-ancestors` other than `'none'`. The pull request check looks at the same form in the build from the list.
- **The check of the answers** — `check`, both after the release and as a command of its own:
  - for `/` and for a random SPA path — the bytes of `index.html`, CSP, `nosniff`, `no-cache` and HSTS;
  - for a file of `assets/` — its bytes, `immutable` and `nosniff`;
  - for an unknown file in `/assets/` — `404`;
  - `/deployment.json` — 200 without a redirect, `application/json`, the bytes from the list;
  - `/NOTICE` and `/LICENSE` — byte for byte the build's files, with `nosniff` and `no-cache`, the shared headers of the paths outside `/assets/**`: the files have no extension, and without `nosniff` a browser would guess their type. The answer's type itself is not compared;
  - nowhere — `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin`.

  It names every wrong answer, not the first. After the release it reads the site in rounds, the first at once and one 10 s after each before, 13 in all, the waits adding up to 120 s; each round checks every answer above, its paths read at once and each within 10 s — a longer read is a wrong answer —, so the check ends within about 250 s. A round still wrong before the last goes to the log with its number and what is wrong, and the last one still wrong refuses with all its wrong answers. The SPA path and the unknown file are new in each round, so no cached answer stands in for them. `just deploy-app-check` is one round. Every line of `release` and `check` goes to the log as it is said.
- **Sign-in** — the access token of `hosting-publish` in `HOSTING_ACCESS_TOKEN`. The recipe `just deploy-app` takes it from `gcloud auth print-access-token` and passes it through the environment, not on the command line; `deploy-app-check` does not go to the API of Hosting and needs no token. The release needs no rights of its own beyond the custom role `hostingPublisher`, and on the project the role works for it, though Firebase writes "Custom roles cannot currently be used for controlling access to Firebase Hosting resources", while the IAM reference marks `firebasehosting.sites.*` as "SUPPORTED" (sources 4.5).
- **Web App** — `google_firebase_web_app` in `tofu/firebase.tf`, `deletion_policy = "PREVENT"` ([`firebase_web_app`](https://github.com/hashicorp/terraform-provider-google-beta/blob/v8.10.0/website/docs/r/firebase_web_app.html.markdown) of `google-beta` at v8.10.0): a new Web App would come with a new key, and a change of the key erases the sign-in on every device, as `app` has it. The output `app_sign_in` is the block `signIn` for `/deployment.json`: `service`, `apiKey`, `authDomain`, `projectId`, `appId`. Firebase creates the key, `apiKeyId`, unless the Web App names one, and restricts it to its own APIs (sources 4.5); on the project it gave the Web App an `apiKeyId` with `apikeys.googleapis.com` off, which the module does not turn on. The author writes the file, and it passes the review of the pull request.
- **The key and the secrets check.** The key is not a secret: every browser that opened `app` gets it in `/deployment.json`, and a key restricted to Firebase's services need not be treated as a secret (sources 4.5). The shared check `Secrets` runs gitleaks, which finds `gcp-api-key` in the list's `deployment.json`. `.gitleaks.toml` at the root extends gitleaks' rules by one exception: a string of the form `AIza…`, and only in `deploy/production/deployment.json`. The check takes `.gitleaks.toml` from the base that the pull request merges into, so the exception comes into `main` by a pull request of its own, before the list. Locally, with it, gitleaks passes the list but still finds the same key in another file and a GitHub token in `deployment.json`.
- **Test** — `tools/deploy/test/app.test.mjs`: a stub API of Hosting on this machine, synthetic releases of `app`, a stub `release-verify` and a stub site, built to answer as the version's configuration has it and broken one answer at a time, and a stand-in time for the rounds.

— *Why:*
- the release is five calls of a documented API, while [`firebase-tools` 15.35.0](https://github.com/firebase/firebase-tools/releases/tag/v15.35.0), since version 15.34.1, asks Service Usage before `deploy` whether the API of Hosting is turned on, and fails without `serviceusage.services.get`, an issue still open (sources 4.5). Besides, it is someone else's large program in a job with the right to release;
- the Hosting emulator is superstatic from `firebase-tools`, while production's path matcher is closed, and nothing says the two agree (sources 4.5). Production's behaviour is checked by the check after the release, on the real site;
- the form of `csp.json` is a contract of the build of `app`, as `spec` decided it, and `app` named it as «одна таблица директив с местами `{api}` и `{authDomain}`» (its [`docs/architecture.md`](https://github.com/altnovatrail/app/blob/375a09b/docs/architecture.md) at 375a09b); its most direct notation is an object;
- without code the Web App would be created by hand, and its `appId` and key must live as long as the project;
- the refusal before the service `api` — a second layer under the hold of `app` in `target.mjs`, which does not roll `app` out while the commit's values do not serve `api`: it also catches a run by hand before the service;
- the rounds — Hosting's CDN serves the build before for a while after a release: a check 19 s after one got the old `index.html` and a 404 for a new file of `assets/`, which answered 200 a minute later. A check that fails on that stops a run of the orchestrator once per release of `app`; 120 s is twice the lag seen, and a site wrong after it is wrong; 10 s reads the site soon after it turns; a read with no bound would hold a round, and the job, as long as the runtime's timeouts allow.

— *Rejected:*
- `firebase-tools` at an exact version — the argument above;
- the Hosting emulator in the devcontainer — the argument above;
- the version's labels instead of the release's message — their allowed form is not checked, and `channels/live` gives the message;
- the Web App as a step of the author in the console;
- waiting for a release of `app` and putting the CSP together later;
- for the key: an exception for the whole file — a secret of another kind in it would pass silently; `.gitleaksignore` with the finding's fingerprint — the fingerprint names the commit, and each later edit of the line would be a finding again; keeping the key outside the repository — `deployment.json` enters the list by sha256 and passes the review;
- for the check after the release: waiting up to 3 minutes — 120 s is already twice the lag seen; a failure rerun by hand — a run of the orchestrator stops on it; the check as a job of its own a minute after `app` — a job and a fixed wait for what a bounded wait in the check does; rounds for `deploy-app-check` too — it reads a site released long before, where a wrong answer is wrong at once.

— *Not checked:* what release a new site has before the rollout's first release.

## The deploy workflow

**R18. The deployment after a merge is done by `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` with four jobs: `target` decides what to deploy, with no access to anything; `config` applies the configuration as `infra-config` in the environment `production-config`; `api` deploys `api` as `api-deploy`, and `app` releases the build as `hosting-publish`, each in the environment that the module's outputs `deploy_environment` and `hosting_environment` name — configuration values, by default `production` and `production-hosting` —, which `config` checks exist and run only from `main`. The deployment goes only while `main` stands on the commit, and the list's parts count as changed against the commit of the last run that deployed — a push to `main`, green, and its job `Configuration` passed — not against the previous push. The tools are installed on the runner at exact versions and sha256 sums, and actionlint checks the workflow.** The model is [`deploy.yml`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/.github/workflows/deploy.yml) of canvas-api.
- **Trigger** — a push to `main` on the paths of what the deployment deploys or what it does it with: `deploy/`, `tofu/` without the tests, `tools/deploy`, `tools/deploy-check`, `tools/ci`, the packages of `tools/`, the `justfile`, `.devcontainer/Dockerfile` — the tools are installed by its `ARG`s — the `spec` submodule — its `release-verify` — the workflow itself and its action. A run by hand, `workflow_dispatch`, applies the configuration and deploys the parts named: `image`, `api`, `facts`, `app`. One queue, `deploy`, without `cancel-in-progress`: a deployment once started is not cut off.
- **`target`** — `tools/deploy/target.mjs`:
  - `main` not on the commit — a green run with a notice: the commit that the branch stands on is deployed by its own run, and the old one would roll production back, as at canvas-api ([`docs/decisions.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/decisions.md) of canvas-api);
  - no `tofu/ci.env`, `tofu/production.auto.tfvars` or `tofu/backend.hcl` — the project is not set up, a green run;
  - no list — only the configuration;
  - otherwise the parts are the blocks `api` and `app` of `deploy/production.json` and `deploy/production/facts.json`, compared by meaning with the commit of the last run of `deploy.yml` that came from a push to `main`, passed, and in which the job `Configuration` passed, out of the last twenty green ones. The part `facts` is also a change of a copy's own legal texts, `deploy/production/legal/`, by the git tree of the folder against the same commit — put in, changed or taken away —, since the deployment lays the folder over the image of `api` by the path of the facts. A run that deployed nothing — `main` moved ahead, or the project is not set up — also ends green, and it does not count as the base. If there is no such run, or its commit is not in the history — all the parts;
  - while `tofu/production.auto.tfvars` does not name `api_image`, `api` has no jobs, since the module creates them only once that names the first image: a changed `api` is only its image, and the facts wait for the service. The run says what comes next: the digest into `api_image`, then `api` by hand;
  - a run by hand does not count as the base: having deployed, say, `app` alone, it would hide an `api` not deployed. What it deployed the next push will deploy again, and that is safe.
- **The guard on a re-run.** "Re-run failed jobs" does not repeat the jobs that the failed one depends on, and their outputs are taken as before (sources 5.5). So `config`, `api` and `app`, on the second and later attempts, first ask `just ci-deploy-current` whether `main` still stands on the commit. On the first attempt the queue suffices, and that `main` only moves forward, as at canvas-api. Each job deploys the commit that `target` decided.
- **`config`** — `just ci-tf-apply`: `init` by the lock file and `backend.hcl`, `apply -auto-approve` waiting for the lock up to five minutes, with the billing account and the operator's address struck from the output. The values are secrets of the environment `production-config`, `TF_VAR_billing_account` and `TF_VAR_operator_email`. The module's outputs, `tofu output -json`, go on as an artifact that lives 30 days — as long as GitHub allows for a re-run — and the service accounts `api-deploy` and `hosting-publish` and the environments `deploy_environment` and `hosting_environment` as the job's outputs, by `ci-tf-outputs`. After `apply` it checks by the GitHub API, `ci-deploy-environments`, that all three environments exist and run only from `main`. The names come from the outputs, not from a parsing of `production.auto.tfvars` of the workflow's own: the source is one, and it is the one that is applied, so a copy with other names gets service accounts that its jobs can take. `production-config` is a constant, as in `bootstrap.sh`.
- **`api`** — `just deploy-api` if the release of `api` changed; otherwise `deploy-api-facts` if the facts changed; by hand also `deploy-api-image`, the image before the jobs, which the module creates only once `api_image` names it. **`app`** — `just deploy-app`, after `api` if that ran, and only if it passed: the new build's `/deployment.json` may name a new `api`.
- **Sign-in** — `google-github-actions/auth` v3.0.0 by SHA: the pool's provider from `tofu/ci.env`, a service account of its own for each job, and `id-token: write` only on the three jobs with an environment. `target` and `config` have `actions: read`: `target` to find the last run that passed, `config` to read the environments.
- **Tools** — `.github/actions/tools` and `tools/ci/install.sh`: OpenTofu, `just` and Node by the `ARG`s of `.devcontainer/Dockerfile`, at the same versions and sums as the container's; the Google Cloud CLI, `jq` and `buildx` at the versions and sums of `tools/ci/versions.env`. The sum is checked before unpacking. Google publishes a sha256 only for the archive with no version in its name, an archive whose content changes with every release, so the sum of the versioned archive is the repository's own, from two downloads that matched (sources 5.5; measurements 4.2). The Docker engine is the runner's: its image pins one version — [`Ubuntu2404-Readme.md`](https://github.com/actions/runner-images/blob/dbf2f009b5e1dcce57cf578ff4fdc1391a4d6d1b/images/ubuntu/Ubuntu2404-Readme.md) and [`toolset-2404.json`](https://github.com/actions/runner-images/blob/dbf2f009b5e1dcce57cf578ff4fdc1391a4d6d1b/images/ubuntu/toolsets/toolset-2404.json) of actions/runner-images at dbf2f00 —, `tools/ci/versions.env` names it, and the job refuses if the engine is another. The job `Deployment tools` in CI installs them all on a real runner — in a pull request that changes what installs them, and on every push to `main` — so a runner with another engine is seen after the nearest merge, before a deployment.
- **Checks without the cloud** — actionlint on the workflows, the container's own; the test of `target.mjs` on repositories made in the test and a stub GitHub API, with the form of the workflow itself: the guard on a re-run in every job after `target`, the environments, `id-token` only there, every action by SHA; the test of `install.sh` on stub `curl` and `docker`.
- **The environments and secrets** are set up by the operator before the first deployment, by the instructions of [architecture.md](architecture.md): `tofu/ci.env`, the three environments with the rule `main`, and the two secrets of `production-config`. An environment that does not exist GitHub creates itself at the first job that names it — without the rule "only `main`" —, and then a workflow of any branch would take its service account: hence the check of `config`. The check `Deploy` is required in the ruleset of `main`, 25506244.
- **Not changed by the review:** a run by hand is no grounds for it — the argument above.
- **Not checked:** whether one must wait until the bindings of the service accounts to the pool take effect (`ci-identity-ready` of canvas-api); what Docker engine the runner has at a deployment.

— *Why:*
- `spec`'s `toolchain.sh` passes into the container neither the Docker socket nor an environment signed in to Google, and without them the deployment would neither build the image nor sign in; a container run of our own beside `spec`'s would repeat its code;
- `setup-gcloud` and `setup-docker-action` do not check the archive ([`setup-gcloud`'s `dist/index.js`](https://github.com/google-github-actions/setup-gcloud/blob/v3.0.1/dist/index.js) at v3.0.1), and Docker does not publish the sums of its static archives (sources 5.5);
- counting from the previous push would lose the changes of a run that a newer one pushed out of the queue, and of a run that failed;
- the parts cannot be learned in full from the state of production: `api-deploy` cannot read the facts, and giving it that right for the sake of counting is needless;
- in `tofu/ci.env` the review sees the two lines;
- the environments from the module's outputs — the module and `bootstrap.sh` take the names from the values, and a workflow with its own strings would leave a copy with other names unable to take its service accounts.

— *Rejected:*
- the tools in the stage `toolchain` and a `docker run` of our own with the socket — the argument above;
- `setup-gcloud` with a version — the archive is not checked;
- counting from `github.event.before` — the argument above;
- by the state of production — the argument above;
- the sign-in values in GitHub variables — they would live in the settings, past review, and neither is a secret;
- the environments' names as strings of the workflow, or parsed from `production.auto.tfvars` — the argument above;
- deploying everything on every run, as canvas-api does — the deployment does only what changed.

— *Consequence:* `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`; `.github/actions/tools/action.yml`; `.github/workflows/ci.yml`; `tools/ci/`; `tools/deploy/target.mjs`; `tools/deploy/test/target.test.mjs`; `tofu/outputs.tf`; `tofu/ci.env`; `justfile`; `.devcontainer/Dockerfile`; `.gitignore`; `docs/architecture.md`.

## The `/deployment.json` format

**R19. The check of a pull request and the release of the build of `app` read `/deployment.json` as `app` reads it: by the reading of the deployment configuration of the submodule's `spec` — its schema, then its rules —, then by the schema of the build, and they refuse a build that reads another version of the format. The eval keys are the module's secrets in the Secret Manager of `infra`, read through their own pool `github-eval`, which `bootstrap.sh` makes.**
- **The format** is `spec`'s deployment configuration, a part of `spec` of its own, as the admission table is: a text, the version — an integer in `VERSION` and in the file's field `format` —, the schema at the path `deployment/schemas/deployment.schema.json`, which does not change from version to version and whose `$id` names the version, examples, reading vectors and the release file `deployment-1.tar.gz`. Version 1 holds `format`, `api` and `signIn`. `csp.json` is no part of the format and stays in the build: `app` fixes its form and the layout of the archive, as the release reads them.
- **The versions.** The build of `app` carries a copy of the schema from the release of `spec` it stands on, and so names the version it reads; the copy is `app`'s to carry, not `infra`'s. A build of another version than the submodule's is refused, naming both: for a higher one `infra` moves to a tag of `spec` of that version, by a task of its own.
- **The reading** — `tools/deploy-check/reading.mjs`, by `spec`'s `deployment/README.md`: the schema, then the rules `deployment.api-origin` and `deployment.loopback` in the order of its table; the first step that finds an error refuses and names the paths of the fields in the order of code units. The rollout is a reader as `app` at its start is, and reads the file so before it writes it: it writes no `http` and checks `deployment.loopback` against the platform's origin of `app`.
- **The checks** — `tools/deploy-check/check.mjs`, three in a row:
  - the file, by the reading of the submodule's `spec`;
  - the build's version, by the `$id` of its schema, against the version of the submodule's `spec`;
  - the file, by the build's schema.
- **The origin of `app`.** In a pull request it is `https://web.app`: the check does not know the site, and the rules look only at whether the host is a loopback one. The release, `tools/deploy/app.mjs`, takes the site's origin from the output `hosting_url` and refuses before the first call to Hosting.
- **The tests:**
  - `tools/deploy-check/test/reading.test.mjs` — each reading vector of the submodule's `spec` gives exactly what is written in it; the vectors are in YAML, read by `yaml` in the `devDependencies` of `tools/package.json`;
  - `check.test.mjs` and `tools/deploy/test/app.test.mjs`: a file that the build's schema lets through and `spec` does not; the rules `deployment.api-origin` and `deployment.loopback`; a build of version 2; a schema without a version.
- **Sign-in** in the production project is set up by `infra`, on Identity Platform; in the dev project, by `api`.
- **The eval keys** — `eval-key-anthropic` and `eval-key-openai`, with user-managed replication in `us-east4` and no values in code. Only the job of the GitHub environment `eval` of the repositories `modules` and `tutor` reads them, by direct Workload Identity Federation, through the pool `github-eval`. The pool and its provider are made by `bootstrap.sh`; the secrets and the rights to them, by the module. The pool of `bootstrap.sh` for `infra`'s own CI and its provider `github` do not change.

— *Why:* checked by the build's schema alone, the format would be held by one side; by the submodule's reading too, the rollout accepts only what `app` accepts, and a build of another version is named before it is released. Where the eval keys lie and how `modules` and `tutor` sign in to read them concern other repositories, so `spec` decides it, and `infra` holds what follows for its own resources. The pool is `bootstrap.sh`'s because the module, to make it, would require for `infra-config` rights over all the project's pools.

— *Rejected:*
- the schema and the vectors from the release file `deployment-1.tar.gz` through `release-verify`: `spec` tells to check by the submodule's schema, whose path does not change, and the submodule stands on a tag checked by its attestation;
- the schema of `spec` alone, without the reading rules. The rollout is a reader too and "MUST NOT accept a file that one step refuses": the schema lets `https://api.example.org:443` through, while `app` refuses it, and after the release the site would not start;
- taking the reading of `spec` itself, `tools/deployment/reading.mjs`, from the submodule: the checks would depend on the inner workings of the tools of `spec`, as the ajv from `spec/tools/node_modules` would, which the check of the list does not take either. The vectors hold the two implementations together;
- a copy of the vectors in `infra`: it would fall behind the tag, while the submodule gives the vectors of the same tag as the schema;
- the pool `github-eval` and its provider made by the module: `infra-config` would need rights over all the project's pools.

— *Consequence:* `tools/deploy-check/`; `tools/deploy/app.mjs`; `tools/deploy/test/app.test.mjs`; `tools/package.json`; `justfile`; `tofu/bootstrap.sh`; `tofu/secrets.tf`; `tofu/eval.tf`; CLAUDE.md; `docs/architecture.md`.

## The eval keys

**R20. The platform's keys for eval are the module's secrets `eval-key-<provider code>`, one per code of `eval_providers`: `eval-key-anthropic` and `eval-key-openai`. Only the job of the GitHub environment `eval` of the repositories of `eval_github_subject_prefixes` — `modules` and `tutor` — reads them, each by its own principal of the pool `github-eval`, with `roles/secretmanager.secretAccessor` and without an intermediary service account. The pool `github-eval` and its provider `github`, which admits only that job and not Dependabot, are made by `bootstrap.sh`, not by the module: `infra-config` has no rights over pools. Without eval repositories there are neither secrets nor a pool, and a pool made before admits nobody.** How it is built is in [architecture.md](architecture.md).
- **The secrets** are in `tofu/secrets.tf`, beside the module's other secrets, and who reads them is in `tofu/eval.tf`:
  - user-managed replication, `var.region`, as for all the module's secrets: a regional secret is read only from its own region (sources 1.5), and a GitHub runner is not there;
  - `labels.purpose = "eval-key"`;
  - `deletion_policy = "ABANDON"`, as for the secrets of the jobs of `api`: a provider that the list no longer names the module forgets, and the value stays with the author;
  - the values are put in by the author, `gcloud secrets versions add`, not by code: no secret's value is in the repository. The spending limits at the providers are set by the author too.
- **Who reads** — each secret gets `secretAccessor` for `principal://iam.googleapis.com/projects/<number>/locations/global/workloadIdentityPools/github-eval/subject/<prefix>:environment:eval`:
  - the prefixes are `eval_github_subject_prefixes`, of the form `repo:OWNER@OWNER_ID/NAME@REPOSITORY_ID`, of one owner, each once. For the platform these are `repo:altnovatrail@344507087/modules@1425775415` and `repo:altnovatrail@344507087/tutor@1425813274`, with `use_immutable_subject: true` (measurements 5.1);
  - by default the list is empty: a self-hosting copy does not need eval;
  - the platform writes it in `tofu/production.auto.tfvars`.
- **The pool and the provider** are made by `bootstrap.sh`, by the function `federated_pool`, shared with the pool of `infra`:
  - the mapping is `google.subject=assertion.sub`;
  - the condition is `assertion.repository_owner_id=='<owner>' && (assertion.repository_id=='<id>' || …) && assertion.environment=='eval' && assertion.actor!='dependabot[bot]'`, and a repeated run puts it back;
  - the script reads the list from the same line of `production.auto.tfvars`, and a list on several lines is a refusal before the first call of `gcloud`;
  - an empty list makes no pool. A pool made before it leaves — the id of a deleted pool cannot be taken again for 30 days —, but it gives that pool's provider the condition `false`, which admits nobody;
  - an edit of the list means running `bootstrap.sh` again before the merge, as an edit of the script itself does: otherwise the module would give the secrets to a principal that the provider's condition does not admit;
  - the pool's id, `github-eval`, is the constant `eval_pool=` of the script and `local.eval_pool` of the module, and the provider's id is `github` in the script's summary line and `local.eval_provider`; `tofu test` holds them equal, and the module and the script reject the repository's own pool under this id;
  - there is no service account in the pool: its principal gets only the eval secrets.
- **The output** `eval_access` is the provider's full name and the secrets by AI provider, what `modules` and `tutor` sign in with; the script prints the line `GCP_EVAL_WORKLOAD_IDENTITY_PROVIDER` too. Without eval the output is `null`.
- **Tests:**
  - `tofu/tests/eval.tftest.hcl` — without repositories there is nothing; with the platform's repositories, two secrets, four `secretAccessor` with exact principals, and the output; repositories without providers, an output without secrets; the refusals of the form, of two owners, of a provider code and of another pool's id; the ids of the pool and the provider are the same as the script's;
  - `tofu/tests/bootstrap.test.mjs` — the condition line for line, the pool of `infra` still admits `infra` alone, without a list the eval pool is not made, and one made before gets `false`, a repeated run puts the condition back, a comment after the list, the refusals of the list's form, of a repeated repository and of two owners.

— *Why not the module:* IAM conditions do not narrow pools — "Resource attributes are available for the Google Cloud services and resource types that are listed on this page. Other services and resource types do not recognize resource attributes", and `iam.googleapis.com` is not on the list (sources 5.6). The right to make the pool `github-eval` would be the right to change the condition of the pool of `bootstrap.sh`, through which `infra-config` itself signs in. And it already lends the deployment service accounts to anyone in the pool (`iam.serviceAccountAdmin`), so it would open production to any repository, while what `infra-config` signs in through is not its to change. The reason for a pool of its own — to separate the pull-request jobs of the two repositories from the deployment service accounts — holds anyway.

— *Rejected:*
- the pool in the module, with `roles/iam.workloadIdentityPoolAdmin` for `infra-config` on the project — see above; only a repeated run of `bootstrap.sh` would put the provider's condition back;
- a custom role of `bootstrap.sh` with only the rights over pools and providers — the rights are still on the whole project, and the risk is the same;
- rights on the pool itself (the pool's `setIamPolicy`): someone has to make the pool before `apply` anyway, that is `bootstrap.sh`, and the provider would then be held by two (sources 5.6);
- the secrets with an empty list too — nobody would read them;
- the repositories' IDs as constants in code: a self-hosting copy could not remove eval without an edit of the module.

— *Consequence:* `tofu/secrets.tf`; `tofu/eval.tf`; `tofu/variables.tf`; `tofu/outputs.tf`; `tofu/bootstrap.sh`; `tofu/production.auto.tfvars`; `tofu/tests/eval.tftest.hcl`; `tofu/tests/bootstrap.test.mjs`; `docs/architecture.md`.

## New releases by the list

**R29. A new release of `api` or `app` goes out by the list when it comes, by a task of its own outside an epic, and waits for no epic; a milestone of an epic of `app` goes out as well. The conditions of the deployment on `novatrail` hold for each one, and the releases are those `deploy/production.json` names.**
- **Why each release.** A module that `api` pins reaches the catalog only with a new release of `api`: the list names no module package, and the build stage of `api` puts them into the image by `modules.json`. A new version of a legal text reaches the learner only with the `app` that shows it, before it applies. A milestone of an epic of `app` goes out so that the course of the epic is seen on the site and its errors are caught early.
- **The pair.** Each is the latest release when its task starts. The workflow ships `app` after `api`, and in between the `app` deployed before calls an `api` whose contract only grew. A release of `app` is read for what it needs of `api` — the methods its `src/` calls outside the tests, `git diff` of its tags over `src/api/`, and the contracts package of its lock file against the contract of `api`'s tag —, and one that needs more than the `api` deployed goes with the `api` that serves it.
- **What comes with a release of `api`** is read before the merge: its migrations, of which database and whether they only add; a new job, required variable or right; the `Dockerfile` against the one deployed; the modules its `modules.json` pins; the operator's commands, which the operator runs as `invite` is run, not the deployment. A release brings its migrations and commands with it in the list, and `api` refuses a variable for development on Cloud Run.
- **The learners' data.** A release that adds a store, a secret, a right, a log or a call that sends a learner's work elsewhere edits BREACH.md, part 1, in the same change; one that adds none leaves it as it is, and its task says so.
- **The checks before the merge.** `deploy-check` on the list: the binary and the `Dockerfile` of `api` verified by `release-verify` and of the sha256 named, the release no lower than the one at base, and the archive of `app` verified and of the sha256 named. `app`'s `csp.json` and `deployment.schema.json` are compared with those deployed, so that the hosting's headers and `deploy/production/deployment.json` change only with them. `api check` of the release, run locally on the packages it lays with the facts, the catalog, the rung and the reading mode of the list, passes, and refuses an ID the pinned index does not hold. Where the parts `deployment/` and `admission/` of `spec` differ between the tag `infra` stands on and the release's, the check of the list by the submodule is read again for the release.
- **The checks after the deployment** are those of a deployment of `api` and `app`, `app`'s `just site-check` of the site and the release among them, and the traces after each deployment of `api` (Д17). What a profile under 13 would be given is checked locally on the packages of the release: there are no children on `novatrail`.

— *Why:* a module that `api` already pins, or a text that `app` must show, reaches the learners only by a deployment, and a release that waits for an epic keeps it from them. Each deployment is one pull request to the list, checked as the list is, and the merge deploys it: a task of its own needs no epic.

— *Rejected:*
- waiting for the next epic, or for the end of an epic of `app`: the learners would wait for what is out, and the course of an epic would not be seen on the site;
- the lowest release that has what is needed, rather than the latest: the next release would deploy the rest anyway;
- `api` alone, without a newer `app` that is out: `app` would be deployed next anyway, and the older one may miss a text or a field that `api` now gives.

— *Consequence:* `deploy/production.json`; `tofu/production.auto.tfvars`; BREACH.md; SELF-HOSTING.md; `docs/architecture.md`.

**R34. `.devcontainer/Dockerfile` has the template's stage `toolchain-core` — Node, just, Go and buf — word for word, and the stage `toolchain` over it adds the three tools of the container, OpenTofu, ShellCheck and actionlint, by a `RUN` of its own.
